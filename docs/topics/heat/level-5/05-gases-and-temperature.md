# Gases and temperature

*Heat and matter · level 5, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

A sealed metal box of air sits on the Mars surface at the Viking 1 site. In the afternoon it is at 242 K; before dawn,
184 K. How much does the pressure inside change?

## The idea

Heat a gas and its particles move faster. What happens next depends on what is held fixed.

**If the volume is fixed** (a sealed rigid box), the pressure rises in step with the kelvin temperature. This is the
**pressure law**:

$$\frac{p_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2}{T_2}.$$

**If the pressure is fixed** (a balloon free to grow), the volume rises in step with the kelvin temperature. This is
**Charles's law**:

$$\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}.$$

Both only work with temperatures in **kelvin**, measured from absolute zero.

## Worked examples

**1. The sealed box.** It was sealed at 101 kPa and 242 K. Before dawn, at 184 K:
$p_2 = 101 \times 184/242 = 77$ kPa, a fall of nearly a quarter, every night.

| Swing in a sealed box of gas, each sol | Mars (Viking 1 site) | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day to night | 242 K to 184 K | suppose 293 K to 278 K |
| Pressure change | about 24% | about 5% |

**2. A balloon at Charles's law.** 3 litres at 300 K is warmed to 330 K at the same pressure: $V_2 = 3 \times 330/300 = 3.3$ litres.

**3. A car tyre.** Its air is at 300 kPa (counting the outside air pressure too, not just what a tyre gauge shows) and
283 K (10 °C). On a hot motorway it warms to 313 K (40 °C): $p_2 = 300 \times 313/283 = 332$ kPa.

## Try it

1. Why must temperatures be in kelvin for these laws?
2. A sealed can of gas at 100 kPa and 300 K is heated to 450 K. Find its pressure.
3. A balloon of 2 litres at 280 K warms to 350 K at the same pressure. Find its volume.
4. What would happen to the pressure of a gas cooled towards 0 K, if it stayed a gas?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The pressure and volume are in proportion to the temperature measured from absolute zero, not from 0 °C.
    2. $100 \times 450/300 = 150$ kPa.
    3. $2 \times 350/280 = 2.5$ litres.
    4. It would fall towards zero, since the particles would almost stop.

## Common mistake

**Using °C.** Warming from 10 °C to 20 °C does not double the pressure. In kelvin it is 283 K to 293 K: a rise of
about 3.5%.
