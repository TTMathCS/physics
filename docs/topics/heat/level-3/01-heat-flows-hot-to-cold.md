# Heat flows from hot to cold

*Heat and matter · level 3, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

You pour a jug of hot water and a jug of cold water into one bowl. What temperature do you end up with?

## The idea

**Heat** is energy moving because of a difference in temperature. It always flows from the **hotter** thing to the
**colder** one, never the other way by itself.

It keeps flowing until both are at the **same temperature**. Then the flow stops. Scientists call this **thermal
equilibrium**.

## Worked examples

**1. Equal jugs.** One litre at 60 °C and one litre at 20 °C, mixed: the hot water cools and the cold water warms. You
end with 2 litres at about **40 °C**, halfway.

**2. Unequal jugs.** One litre at 60 °C and three litres at 20 °C: the cold water has more to warm up, so you end
closer to 20 °C. The answer is 30 °C: $\dfrac{1\times60 + 3\times20}{4} = 30$.

**3. A rover at night.** Inside, its electronics box is warm; outside, Mars is far below freezing. All night, heat flows
out of the box into the cold. Heaters must keep putting heat back in.

## Try it

1. Which way does heat flow between a hot drink and the air?
2. When does the flow stop?
3. Mix 2 litres at 50 °C with 2 litres at 10 °C. What temperature do you get?
4. Why can an ice cube never make a warm drink hotter?

??? note "Answers"
    1. From the hot drink to the cooler air.
    2. When both are at the same temperature.
    3. Halfway: 30 °C.
    4. Heat flows from the warm drink into the colder ice, so the drink cools.

## Common mistake

**"Cold flows into things."** Only heat flows. A fridge feels cold because heat flows out of your hand into it.
