# Insulators

*Heat and matter · level 3, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

A vacuum flask keeps soup hot all day, and keeps juice cold too. How does one flask do both?

## The idea

An **insulator** slows heat down. It does not stop heat completely, but it makes it leak far more slowly.

The best insulators trap lots of **still air** in tiny pockets: wool, feathers, foam, fluffy fibres. Air conducts heat
badly, and when it is trapped it cannot move around and carry heat away.

A **vacuum flask** is even better. It has two walls with **nothing**, a vacuum, between them:

- With no particles in the gap, heat cannot **conduct** across it, or be carried by moving air.
- The walls are **shiny**, so they reflect heat that tries to cross as radiation.
- The **stopper** stops warm air escaping from the top.

It works both ways: heat cannot easily get **out** of hot soup, or **into** cold juice.

## Worked examples

**1. A woolly hat.** Wool traps air between its fibres. Your head's heat leaks out slowly.

**2. Loft insulation.** Thick fluffy layers in a roof trap air, so less of the house's heat escapes.

**3. A spacesuit.** It has many thin layers, with gaps between them, to keep heat in on a cold Mars night.

## Try it

1. What does an insulator do?
2. Why are fluffy materials good insulators?
3. Why is there a vacuum between the walls of a flask?
4. Why can a flask keep cold drinks cold?

??? note "Answers"
    1. It slows down the flow of heat.
    2. They trap lots of still air, which conducts heat badly.
    3. Without particles, heat cannot be conducted or carried across.
    4. It slows heat getting in from the warm air, as well as heat getting out.

## Common mistake

**"Insulators make things warm."** An insulator only slows heat flow. Wrap an ice cube in a blanket and it stays cold
for longer.
