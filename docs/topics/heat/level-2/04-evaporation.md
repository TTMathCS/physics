# Evaporation

*Heat and matter · level 2, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

After the rain, there are puddles everywhere. A day later they are gone. Nobody boiled them. Where did the water go?

## The idea

Water can turn into a gas without boiling. This is called **evaporation**. It happens slowly, at the **surface** of the
water, at any temperature. The water goes into the air as **water vapour**, a gas you cannot see.

Water evaporates **faster** when:

- it is **warm**, such as on a sunny day;
- it is **windy**, so the damp air is blown away;
- it is **spread out**, so more of it touches the air.

## Worked examples

**1. Washing on a line.** It dries fastest on a warm, windy day.

**2. Wet hair.** A hairdryer blows warm air, so the water evaporates quickly.

**3. A spilt drink.** Spread out on the table, it dries faster than the same drink in a cup.

## Try it

1. What is evaporation?
2. Do puddles need to boil to dry up?
3. Give two things that make washing dry faster.
4. Why does a towel spread out dry faster than one in a heap?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A liquid slowly turning into a gas at its surface.
    2. No. They evaporate.
    3. Warmth, wind (also spreading it out).
    4. More of the water touches the air.

## Common mistake

**"The water is gone for ever."** It is in the air as water vapour. Later it can come back as rain or dew.
