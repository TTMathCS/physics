# Boiling and steam

*Heat and matter · level 2, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

Heat a pan of water and, after a while, big bubbles rise and burst. What is inside those bubbles?

## The idea

When water gets hot enough, it **boils**. Bubbles of **steam** form deep in the water, rise and burst at the top.
Steam is water as a **gas**.

In a pan in your kitchen, water boils at about **100 °C**. However long you keep heating it, boiling water does not get
any hotter; the heat goes into making more steam.

You cannot see real steam. The white cloud above a kettle is made of tiny drops of water: the steam has cooled in the
air and turned back into liquid.

**Be careful.** Steam and boiling water burn badly. Only grown-ups should handle a boiling kettle.

## Worked examples

**1. A kettle.** It heats water to 100 °C. The water boils, and the kettle switches itself off.

**2. Pasta.** Cooks boil it in water at 100 °C. Turning the heat up only makes the water boil harder, not hotter.

**3. The white cloud.** Look closely at a kettle's spout. Right at the spout there is a clear gap: real steam. The cloud
starts a little way off.

## Try it

1. What is steam?
2. At about what temperature does water boil in a pan?
3. What is the white cloud above a kettle made of?
4. Why is steam dangerous?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Water as a gas.
    2. About 100 °C.
    3. Tiny drops of liquid water.
    4. It is very hot and can burn your skin.

## Common mistake

**"The white cloud is steam."** Real steam is invisible. The cloud is steam that has already turned back into tiny
drops of water.
