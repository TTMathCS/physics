# Solids, liquids and gases

*Heat and matter · level 2, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A brick, a glass of juice and the air in a balloon. What makes each one different?

## The idea

Everything around you is a **solid**, a **liquid** or a **gas**. These are the three **states of matter**.

| State | Shape | Does it flow? | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solid | Keeps its own shape | No | Ice, wood, a brick, a spoon |
| Liquid | Takes the shape of its container | Yes, it pours | Water, milk, juice, oil |
| Gas | Spreads out to fill any space | Yes, everywhere | Air, steam, the gas in a balloon |

Everything is made of tiny **particles**, far too small to see.

- In a **solid**, the particles are packed in neat rows and hold on tight. They only jiggle on the spot.
- In a **liquid**, they are still close, but can slide past each other.
- In a **gas**, they are far apart and zoom about in all directions.

## Worked examples

**1. Juice.** Pour it from a carton into a glass. It takes the shape of the glass. It is a liquid.

**2. A balloon.** The air inside spreads out to fill it. Air is a gas.

**3. Sand.** It pours, but each grain is a tiny solid that keeps its own shape. Sand is a solid made of lots of bits.

## Try it

1. Is milk a solid, a liquid or a gas?
2. Is a pencil a solid, a liquid or a gas?
3. What does a gas do when you let it out of a bottle?
4. In which state are the particles furthest apart?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A liquid.
    2. A solid.
    3. It spreads out into the room.
    4. In a gas.

## Common mistake

**"Gases are not really anything."** Air is real stuff. You feel it in the wind, and it fills a balloon. Its particles
are just far apart.
