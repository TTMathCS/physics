# Heat and matter

Mars is cold: about −60 °C on average, and the air is so thin that water boils away at a few degrees above
freezing. This topic starts with hot and cold and climbs to temperature scales, heat flow, the gas laws and phase
diagrams, the physics behind keeping people warm and water liquid on Mars.

8 levels. The ages are only a rough guide for parents and teachers; anyone can start at any level.

| Level | Rough age | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1: Hot and cold | 5-6 | Feeling hot and cold, thermometers, keeping warm |
| Level 2: Solid, liquid, gas | 7-8 | Melting, freezing, boiling, ice and steam |
| Level 3: Heat on the move | 9-10 | Conduction, convection, radiation; why a blanket works |
| Level 4: Temperature, measured | 11-12 | Celsius and kelvin, specific heat, the cold of a Mars night |
| Level 5: Pressure and gases | 12-13 | Pressure as force per area, the 6 hPa air of Mars, gas laws |
| Level 6: Phase changes | 13-14 | Latent heat, why water boils at low pressure, the triple point |
| Level 7: Insulating a home | 15-16 | Heat loss through walls, regolith shielding as insulation, a heating budget |
| Level 8: Thermodynamics | 17 and beyond | The laws of thermodynamics, heat pumps, engines and the climate of Mars |
