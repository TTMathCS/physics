# Thermometers

*Heat and matter · level 1, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Your hands can tell hot from cold, but not exactly how hot. How do doctors and weather forecasters know?

## The idea

A **thermometer** tells you how hot or cold something is. The number is the **temperature**, and we often measure it in
**degrees Celsius**, written °C.

- **0 °C** is the temperature where water freezes and ice melts.
- **Below 0** the numbers have a minus sign: −10 °C is colder than 0 °C.
- **100 °C** is where water boils in a pan.

## Worked examples

**1. Your body.** Inside, you are about 37 °C. A doctor checks this when you are ill.

**2. A warm day.** The weather forecast says 25 °C. That is warm enough for shorts.

**3. Ice.** It is 0 °C or colder. Ice that is warmer than 0 °C melts.

## Try it

1. What does a thermometer measure?
2. Which is colder, 5 °C or −5 °C?
3. Which is hotter, a room at 20 °C or a bath at 40 °C?
4. At what temperature does ice melt?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Temperature: how hot or cold something is.
    2. −5 °C.
    3. The bath.
    4. 0 °C.

## Common mistake

**"−10 is bigger than −5."** With a minus sign, the bigger number is colder. −10 °C is colder than −5 °C.
