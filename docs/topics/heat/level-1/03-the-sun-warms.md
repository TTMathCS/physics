# The Sun warms things

*Heat and matter · level 1, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

On a sunny day, why does a bench in the sunshine feel warm and the one in the shade feel cool?

## The idea

Sunlight brings **warmth**. Things the Sun shines on get warmer. In the **shade**, something blocks the sunlight, so it
stays cooler.

**Dark things** get warmer in the Sun than light-coloured things. A black car on a sunny day gets hotter than a white
one.

**On Mars**, the Sun warms the ground too. At midday near the middle of Mars, the ground can be as warm as a spring day,
but the thin air above it stays freezing. If you stood there, your feet would feel spring and your head would feel
winter.

## Worked examples

**1. A sunny wall.** Put your hand on a wall the Sun has shone on all morning. It feels warm.

**2. A shady wall.** The wall on the other side of the house feels cooler.

**3. Two T-shirts.** A black T-shirt in the sunshine feels hotter than a white one.

## Try it

1. Why is it cooler in the shade?
2. Which gets hotter in the Sun, a black car or a white car?
3. Where would you sit on a hot day to stay cool?
4. On Mars at midday, which is warmer: the ground or the air at your head?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Something, such as a tree or a roof, blocks the sunlight.
    2. The black car.
    3. In the shade.
    4. The ground.

## Common mistake

**"The Sun only gives light."** It gives warmth too. That is why things in the sunshine get warm.
