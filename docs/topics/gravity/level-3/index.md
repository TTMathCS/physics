# Level 3: Falling, measured

*Gravity and orbits · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In this level you put numbers on falling. You time a fall and find that a falling thing gains the same speed every
second, see how air holds things back and why parachutes work, trace the curve of a thrown ball, and find out what
it would be like to live in 0.38 g, the pull of Mars. It ends with the tides, gravity stretching a whole ocean.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Timing a fall](01-timing-a-fall.md) | Use "about 10 m/s faster every second" to find a falling thing's speed, on Earth and on Mars |
| [Air gets in the way](02-air-gets-in-the-way.md) | Explain air resistance and why a falling thing stops speeding up |
| [Parachutes](03-parachutes.md) | Explain how a parachute slows a fall, and why Mars needs very big ones |
| [The shape of a throw](04-the-shape-of-a-throw.md) | Describe the curve of a throw, find the best angle, and compare throws on two planets |
| [A world at 0.38 g](05-a-world-at-0-38-g.md) | Say what "g" means and how walking, pouring, building and the human body would change on Mars |
| [Tides](06-tides.md) | Explain why the sea has two high tides a day, and what Phobos does to Mars |
