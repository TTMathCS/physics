# The shape of a throw

*Gravity and orbits · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

You want to throw a ball as far as you can across a field. Should you throw it flat, straight up, or somewhere in
between?

## The idea

A thrown ball does two things at once:

- It moves **across** at a steady speed (if the air does not slow it much).
- It moves **up and down** like a ball thrown straight up: slowing on the way up, stopping, speeding up on the way down.

Put the two together and you get a smooth arch called a **parabola**. Every thrown ball, every jet from a fountain
and every jumping frog follows one.

At the same throwing speed:

- Thrown **flat**, the ball moves across fast but lands almost at once.
- Thrown **straight up**, it stays in the air a long time but goes nowhere across.
- In between is best. Without air, the farthest throw is at **45°**, halfway between flat and straight up. Throws at
  30° and 60° land at the same spot, a bit short of the 45° one.

**On Mars**, gravity pulls about a third as hard, so the ball stays up longer and keeps moving across for longer.
The same throw goes about **2.6 times as far**. A ball you can throw 30 m at home would go nearly 80 m on Mars.

## Worked examples

**1. A long jumper.** Long jumpers take off at about 20°, not 45°: they run so fast that they cannot push up hard
enough for a steep jump. They still trace a parabola.

**2. A garden hose.** Point the hose flat, then at 45°, then nearly straight up, without changing the tap. The 45°
jet lands farthest.

## Try it

1. What is the shape of a throw called?
2. At what angle does a throw go farthest, without air?
3. Two throws at the same speed, at 30° and 60°. Which goes farther?
4. A ball you throw 20 m on Earth: about how far on Mars?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A parabola.
    2. 45°.
    3. They land at the same distance (without air). The 60° one goes higher.
    4. About $20 \times 2.6 = 52$ m.

## Common mistake

**"The ball flies straight, then drops."** Cartoon characters run off cliffs and then fall. Real balls start curving
the moment they leave your hand, because gravity pulls on them all the time.
