# Timing a fall

*Gravity and orbits · level 3, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Drop a ball from your hand. At first it is slow; by the time it hits the floor it is moving quite fast. How much
faster does it get, and how quickly?

## The idea

Scientists have timed falling things very carefully. Without air in the way, every falling thing near the Earth's
surface gets faster by the same amount every second:

> **About 10 metres per second faster, every second.** (More exactly 9.8.)

That steady speeding up is called **acceleration**. Here it is, second by second, for a stone dropped from a very
high cliff (ignoring the air):

| Time after letting go | Speed on Earth | Speed on Mars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 s | 0 m/s | 0 m/s |
| 1 s | 10 m/s | 3.7 m/s |
| 2 s | 20 m/s | 7.4 m/s |
| 3 s | 30 m/s | 11.1 m/s |
| 4 s | 40 m/s | 14.8 m/s |

On Mars a falling thing gains only 3.7 m/s every second. It still speeds up steadily, but more gently.

How far does it go? In the first second on Earth it falls about 5 m. By the end of 2 seconds, about 20 m; by 3
seconds, 45 m. Divide by 5 and you get 1, 4, 9: the **square numbers**. The distance grows faster and faster because
the speed keeps growing. On Mars it falls about 1.9 m in the first second, 7.4 m in two, and 17 m in three.

## Worked examples

**1. The well.** A stone dropped into a well hits the water after 2 seconds. About how fast is it going? $2 \times 10 =
20$ m/s. How deep is the well? About 20 m.

**2. A rock on Mars.** A rock falls for 3 seconds on Mars. Its speed is $3 \times 3.7 = 11.1$ m/s. On Earth, after 3
seconds, it would be going 30 m/s, nearly three times as fast.

**3. The ruler test.** Hold a ruler at the top, with a friend's open fingers at the bottom. Let go without warning.
The farther it falls before they catch it, the slower their reactions. A catch at 20 cm means about 0.2 s.

## Try it

1. A ball is dropped on Earth. How fast is it going after 5 seconds (ignoring air)?
2. The same ball on Mars: how fast after 5 seconds?
3. How far does a stone fall on Earth in 4 seconds? (Use the square numbers.)
4. A ball is going 30 m/s when it hits the ground on Earth. How long was it falling?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $5 \times 10 = 50$ m/s.
    2. $5 \times 3.7 = 18.5$ m/s.
    3. $5 \times 16 = 80$ m (more exactly 78 m).
    4. 3 seconds.

## Common mistake

**"It falls at 10 m/s."** It does not fall at one speed. It gains 10 m/s **every second**: 10 after one second, 20
after two, 30 after three. The 10 is how fast the speed grows, not the speed itself.
