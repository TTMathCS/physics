# Level 1: Up and down

*Gravity and orbits · level 1 of 8 · rough age 5-6 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Everything you let go of falls down. In this level you find out what "down" means on a round planet, test whether
heavy things fall faster than light ones, throw and catch, and see how high you could jump on Mars. It ends with a
puzzle: if everything falls, why does the Moon stay up?

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Why things fall down](01-things-fall-down.md) | Say that the Earth pulls things towards its middle, and that this pull is called gravity |
| [Heavy and light](02-heavy-and-light.md) | Test with two balls that heavy and light things fall together, and say why a feather is slower |
| [Throwing and catching](03-throwing-and-catching.md) | Describe the curved path of a thrown ball and why it always comes back down |
| [Round planets](04-round-planets.md) | Explain why "down" points to the middle of the planet, wherever you stand |
| [Jumping on Mars](05-jumping-on-mars.md) | Say that Mars pulls less, so you would jump almost three times as high |
| [The Moon goes round](06-the-moon-goes-round.md) | Explain that the Moon is falling all the time, but moving sideways so fast that it keeps missing |
