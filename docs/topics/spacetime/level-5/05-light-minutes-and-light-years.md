# Light-minutes and light-years

*Space and time · level 5, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

A light-year sounds like a length of time. It is not. What is it?

## The idea

Space distances get so big that kilometres stop being handy. So astronomers often give a distance as **how long light
takes to cross it**.

- A **light-second** is about 300,000 km.
- A **light-minute** is $60 \times 300{,}000 = 18$ million km.
- A **light-year** is the distance light goes in a year: about **9.46 trillion km**.

The Moon is about 1.3 light-seconds away. The nearest star beyond the Sun, Proxima Centauri, is just over 4
light-years away.

| Distance from the Sun | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In light-minutes | about 12.7 | about 8.3 |
| In au | about 1.52 | 1 |

## Worked examples

**1. A light-year.** A year is about 31.6 million seconds. $31{,}600{,}000 \times 300{,}000 \approx 9.5$ trillion km.

**2. Mars in light-minutes.** 228 million km $\div$ 18 million km $\approx 12.7$ light-minutes.

**3. Proxima in km.** It is just over 4 light-years away. $4 \times 9.46$ trillion $\approx 38$ trillion km, so a
little more than that.

## Try it

1. Is a light-year a time or a distance?
2. About how many km is a light-minute?
3. Jupiter is about 778 million km from the Sun. How many light-minutes is that?
4. Why do astronomers use light-years for stars?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A distance: how far light goes in a year.
    2. About 18 million km.
    3. $778 \div 18 \approx 43$ light-minutes.
    4. Star distances in km are huge, hard-to-read numbers.

## Common mistake

**"A light-year is a year."** It is a distance, about 9.46 trillion km.
