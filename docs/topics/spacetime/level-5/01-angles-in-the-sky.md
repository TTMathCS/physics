# Angles in the sky

*Space and time · level 5, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Hold your little finger out at arm's length. Can it cover the full Moon?

## The idea

Astronomers cannot measure the sky in metres, because they do not know how far away things are. So they measure
**angles**.

- A full circle round the sky is **360°**. From the horizon to straight overhead is **90°**.
- Each degree splits into **60 arcminutes** (60′).
- Each arcminute splits into **60 arcseconds** (60″). So $1° = 3{,}600″$.

The Sun and the Moon both look about **half a degree** wide, about 31′. Planets look far smaller: Mars is never more
than about 26″ across, so small that it looks like a dot without a telescope.

## Worked examples

**1. Converting.** The Moon is about 0.52° wide. $0.52 \times 60 \approx 31$ arcminutes.

**2. Mars at its biggest.** 26″. $26 \div 3{,}600 \approx 0.0072°$, about 70 times smaller than the Moon.

**3. Your finger.** A finger about 1.5 cm wide, held 60 cm from your eye, covers about
$57.3 \times \frac{1.5}{60} \approx 1.4°$. That is nearly three full Moons. (Where 57.3 comes from is in the next lesson.)

## Try it

1. How many arcseconds are in one degree?
2. About how wide do the Sun and the Moon look?
3. Convert 2° to arcminutes.
4. Could your little finger at arm's length cover the full Moon?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 3,600.
    2. About half a degree each.
    3. $2 \times 60 = 120′$.
    4. Yes, easily: a finger covers more than 1°, and the Moon is only about 0.5°.

## Common mistake

**"The Moon looks as big as a plate."** It covers only half a degree, less than your finger at arm's length. It just
seems large near the horizon.
