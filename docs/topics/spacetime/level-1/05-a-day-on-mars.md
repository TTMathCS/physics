# A day on Mars

*Space and time · level 1, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Mars spins round too, so it has days and nights. Is a day on Mars the same length as a day on Earth?

## The idea

Almost. Mars spins a little more slowly than Earth. From one sunrise to the next takes about **24 hours and 40
minutes**: about 40 minutes longer than a day on Earth.

A day on Mars has its own name: a **sol**.

| A day | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | a sol | a day |
| Length | about 24 hours 40 minutes | 24 hours |

The people who drive rovers on Mars sometimes live on Mars time. Each day they start work 40 minutes later than the day
before.

## Worked examples

**1. A little longer.** A sol is about 40 minutes longer than an Earth day.

**2. Two sols.** After two sols, Mars time is about 80 minutes, nearly an hour and a half, behind Earth time.

**3. Starting late.** A rover team on Mars time that starts at 8 o'clock today starts at about 8:40 tomorrow.

## Try it

1. What is a day on Mars called?
2. Is a sol longer or shorter than an Earth day?
3. By about how much?
4. Why do some rover teams start work later each day?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A sol.
    2. Longer.
    3. About 40 minutes.
    4. They are living on Mars time, and each sol is about 40 minutes longer than an Earth day.

## Common mistake

**"Every planet has a 24-hour day."** Each planet spins at its own speed. Mars's day is close to ours, but Jupiter's is
under 10 hours.
