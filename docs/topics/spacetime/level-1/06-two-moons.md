# Two moons

*Space and time · level 1, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

We have one Moon. How many does Mars have, and what would they look like from the ground?

## The idea

Mars has **two** small moons, called **Phobos** and **Deimos**. They are not round like our Moon; they are lumpy, like
potatoes. Phobos is about 27 km long, smaller than a city.

Phobos is very close to Mars and very fast. It goes round Mars **three times a day**. It goes round faster than Mars
spins, so it does something strange: it **rises in the west** and sets in the east, the opposite way to the Sun.

Deimos is smaller and farther away. It goes round more slowly, so it drifts slowly across the sky.

| Moons | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many | 2 | 1 |
| Biggest | Phobos, about 27 km long | the Moon, 3,475 km across |

## Worked examples

**1. Three times.** Phobos rises and sets about twice each sol, as seen from the ground.

**2. Size.** Our Moon is about 3,475 km across. Phobos is about 27 km long: more than a hundred times smaller.

**3. The other way.** The Sun rises in the east on Mars, just as on Earth. Phobos rises in the west.

## Try it

1. How many moons does Mars have?
2. What are their names?
3. Which of them goes round Mars three times a day?
4. Where does Phobos rise?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Two.
    2. Phobos and Deimos.
    3. Phobos.
    4. In the west.

## Common mistake

**"All moons are round."** Our Moon is big enough that its own gravity has pulled it round. Phobos and Deimos are too
small, so they stay lumpy.
