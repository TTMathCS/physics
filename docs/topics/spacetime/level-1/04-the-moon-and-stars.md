# The Moon and stars

*Space and time · level 1, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

Some nights the Moon is a big round ball. Other nights it is a thin curve, like a banana. Is the Moon really changing
shape?

## The idea

No. The Moon is always a round ball. It does not make its own light; it shines because the **Sun lights it up**.

The Moon goes round the Earth. As it does, we see different amounts of its sunny side:

- When we see the whole sunny side, there is a **full Moon**.
- When we see only a sliver, there is a thin **crescent**.
- When the sunny side faces away from us, we can hardly see the Moon at all: a **new Moon**.

From one full Moon to the next takes about **29 and a half days**: about a month. The word "month" comes from "Moon".

The **stars** are suns, very far away. They are in the sky in the daytime too, but the Sun is so bright we cannot see
them.

## Worked examples

**1. A month.** From one full Moon to the next is about 29 and a half days.

**2. Two weeks.** Half of that, about 15 days, takes the Moon from new to full.

**3. Daytime Moon.** Sometimes you can see the Moon in the daytime too, pale in the blue sky.

## Try it

1. Does the Moon make its own light?
2. About how long is it from one full Moon to the next?
3. What is a thin Moon called?
4. Why can we not see stars in the daytime?

??? note "Answers"
    1. No. The Sun lights it up.
    2. About 29 and a half days.
    3. A crescent.
    4. The Sun is so bright that it hides them.

## Common mistake

**"The Earth's shadow makes the Moon's shapes."** The shapes come from how much of the Moon's sunny side we can see. The
Earth's shadow only falls on the Moon during an eclipse.
