# A model solar system

*Space and time · level 2, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

Books often draw the planets as big balls, close together round the Sun. What would a model look like if everything
were the right size and the right distance apart?

## The idea

Shrink everything by the same amount: say, 10 billion times. Then the Sun becomes a ball about **14 cm** across, the
size of a grapefruit. Everything else shrinks too:

| Object | Size in the model | Distance from the Sun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | a grapefruit, 14 cm | in the middle |
| Earth | a pinhead, 1.3 mm | 15 m |
| Moon | a speck, 0.35 mm | 4 cm from the Earth |
| Mars | a grain of sand, 0.7 mm | 23 m |
| Jupiter | a grape, 14 mm | 78 m |
| Neptune | a pea, 5 mm | 452 m |

The Earth is a pinhead 15 big steps from the grapefruit. Mars is a grain of sand 23 m away. Neptune is nearly half a
kilometre off. Space is mostly **empty**.

The distances along the path are to scale; the balls are drawn bigger so you can see them.

## Worked examples

**1. Shrinking the Sun.** The Sun is 1,392,000 km across. Shrunk 10 billion times: 139 mm, about 14 cm.

**2. Shrinking the distance.** The Earth is 150 million km from the Sun. Shrunk 10 billion times: 15 m.

**3. Mars.** 228 million km shrinks to about 23 m. Mars is a tiny grain only 8 m beyond the Earth's pinhead.

## Try it

1. In the model, how big is the Sun?
2. How far from the Sun is the Earth in the model?
3. Which is farther in the model: Mars at 23 m or Jupiter at 78 m?
4. What does the model show about space?

??? note "Answers"
    1. About 14 cm, like a grapefruit.
    2. About 15 m.
    3. Jupiter.
    4. That it is mostly empty: tiny planets with huge gaps between them.

## Common mistake

**"Pictures in books are to scale."** To fit on a page, books draw the planets far too big and far too close. Real space
is mostly empty.
