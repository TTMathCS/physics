# A message to Mars

*Space and time · level 3, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

You send a "hello" by radio to a friend on Mars. How long before they hear it? How long before you hear their reply?

## The idea

Radio travels at the speed of light. But Mars is so far away that even light takes minutes:

- When Mars is **closest**, about 54.6 million km away: about **3 minutes**.
- When Mars is **farthest**, about 401 million km away: about **22 minutes**.

A reply takes just as long to come back. So a chat with Mars has a long, awkward pause in it: from about 6 minutes to
about 45 minutes between saying "hello" and hearing "hello" back.

## Worked examples

**1. Closest.** $54{,}600{,}000 \div 300{,}000 = 182$ seconds: about 3 minutes.

**2. Farthest.** $401{,}000{,}000 \div 300{,}000 \approx 1{,}337$ seconds: about 22 minutes.

**3. A middle distance.** Suppose Mars is 225 million km away. $225{,}000{,}000 \div 300{,}000 = 750$ seconds: 12 and a
half minutes each way, 25 minutes there and back.

## Try it

1. About how long does a message take to reach Mars at its closest?
2. And at its farthest?
3. Why can you not have a normal phone call with someone on Mars?
4. Mars is 90 million km away. How long does a message take?

??? note "Answers"
    1. About 3 minutes.
    2. About 22 minutes.
    3. Each message takes minutes to arrive, so every reply comes much later.
    4. $90{,}000{,}000 \div 300{,}000 = 300$ seconds: 5 minutes.

## Common mistake

**"The delay is always the same."** It changes all the time, from about 3 minutes to about 22, as Earth and Mars move
round the Sun.
