# Seasons of unequal length

*Space and time · level 4, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

On Earth the four seasons are all about three months long. On Mars, northern spring is about 50 sols longer than
northern autumn. Why?

## The idea

Mars has seasons for the same reason Earth does: its axis is tilted, by about **25.2°** (Earth's is about 23.4°). But
Mars's orbit is more stretched. Its distance from the Sun swings from about **207 million km** to about **249 million
km**, and it moves faster when it is close to the Sun.

Mars is farthest from the Sun, and slowest, near the start of northern summer. So the northern spring and summer drag on,
and the autumn and winter rush by.

Scientists mark the time of a Mars year by **Ls**, the angle Mars has gone round the Sun since the start of northern
spring. Ls = 0° starts spring, 90° summer, 180° autumn and 270° winter.

| Northern season (Ls) | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring (0° to 90°) | about 193 sols | about 93 days |
| Summer (90° to 180°) | about 179 sols | about 94 days |
| Autumn (180° to 270°) | about 143 sols | about 90 days |
| Winter (270° to 360°) | about 154 sols | about 89 days |

The south gets the opposite seasons: its summer is short and hot, close to the Sun. The dust storm season begins just
after Mars passes closest to the Sun, at about Ls = 260°.

## Worked examples

**1. Spring's share.** $193 \div 668.6 \approx 0.29$: northern spring is about 29% of the Mars year. With four equal
seasons it would be 25%.

**2. Longest minus shortest.** $193 - 143 = 50$ sols.

**3. Reading Ls.** Mars is at Ls = 135°. That is halfway between 90° and 180°: the middle of northern summer.

## Try it

1. Why does Mars have seasons?
2. Which northern season on Mars is longest? Which is shortest?
3. What does Ls = 270° mean?
4. Why is the southern summer on Mars short and hot?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Its axis is tilted, like Earth's.
    2. Spring is longest (about 193 sols); autumn is shortest (about 143 sols).
    3. The start of northern winter.
    4. It happens when Mars is closest to the Sun, moving fastest and getting the most sunlight.

## Common mistake

**"Seasons come from being nearer the Sun."** The tilt makes the seasons. The changing distance only makes them unequal,
and stronger in one hemisphere.
