# The Mars year

*Space and time · level 4, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

You are 12 years old on Earth. How old would you be in Mars years? How often would you get a birthday?

## The idea

Mars is farther from the Sun than Earth, and it moves more slowly, so it takes longer to go round. One trip, a Mars year,
lasts about **687 Earth days**, or **668.6 sols**.

| One year | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In Earth days | about 687 | about 365.25 |
| In the planet's own days | about 668.6 sols | about 365.25 days |
| In Earth years | about 1.88 | 1 |

Scientists number Mars years too. **Mars Year 1** began on 11 April 1955, at the start of northern spring on Mars. The
date was picked so that a global dust storm watched in 1956 fell in Year 1. **Mars Year 39** began on 30 September 2026.

## Worked examples

**1. Years.** $687 \div 365.25 \approx 1.88$: a Mars year is nearly two Earth years.

**2. Your age.** $12 \times 365.25 = 4{,}383$ Earth days. $4{,}383 \div 687 \approx 6.4$ Mars years.

**3. Birthdays.** A Mars birthday comes once every 687 Earth days, so about once every 1 year 10 months.

## Try it

1. About how many Earth days are in a Mars year?
2. About how many sols are in a Mars year?
3. When did Mars Year 1 begin?
4. Someone is 30 Earth years old. About how old are they in Mars years?

??? note "Answers"
    1. About 687.
    2. About 668.6.
    3. 11 April 1955.
    4. $30 \div 1.88 \approx 16$ Mars years.

## Common mistake

**Mixing days and sols.** A Mars year is 687 *Earth days* but 668.6 *sols*. Say which one you mean.
