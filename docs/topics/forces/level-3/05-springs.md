# Springs that measure force

*Forces and motion · level 3, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Hang a small weight on a spring, and it stretches. Hang two, and it stretches more. How much more? Could you use this
to measure forces?

## The idea

Pull on a spring, and it stretches. For most springs, as long as you don't pull too hard:

**Twice the force gives twice the stretch. Three times the force, three times the stretch.**

This rule was found by Robert Hooke in 1660, and is called **Hooke's law**. It means a spring can measure forces: mark
how far it stretches for 1 N, 2 N, 3 N, and you have a scale.

That is a **newton meter**, or **spring balance**: a spring in a tube with a scale of newtons beside it, and a hook at
the bottom.

If you pull **too** hard, past the spring's **elastic limit**, it stays stretched when you let go and the rule no longer
works. A spring scale must never be overloaded.

Springs are everywhere in machines: in pens, car wheels, mattresses and clocks. The landing legs of many landers have
springs or crushable parts inside to soak up the bump of touchdown. A Mars rover is an exception: its wheels hang from
levers and joints, the **rocker-bogie**, with no springs at all, so it rolls slowly and evenly over rocks without
bouncing.

## Worked examples

**1. Reading a spring.** A spring stretches 2 cm for each 1 N. A rock makes it stretch 7 cm. The force is
$7 \div 2 = 3.5$ N.

**2. Too much?** The same spring's elastic limit is at 10 N. A 15 N load would bend it out of shape for good.

## Try it

1. A spring stretches 3 cm with 1 N. How far does it stretch with 4 N?
2. A spring stretches 5 cm with 2 N. What force stretches it 10 cm?
3. What is the elastic limit?
4. What instrument uses a spring to measure force?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 12 cm.
    2. 4 N.
    3. The largest force a spring can take and still go back to its own length; past it, it stays stretched.
    4. A newton meter (spring balance).

## Common mistake

**"The spring's length doubles when the force doubles."** It is the **stretch**, the extra length, that doubles. A
10 cm spring that stretches to 12 cm with 1 N is 14 cm long with 2 N, not 24 cm.
