# The newton

*Forces and motion · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

We measure length in metres and mass in kilograms. What do we measure force in? How big is one?

## The idea

Force is measured in **newtons**, written **N**, after Isaac Newton, who worked out the laws of motion in the 1680s.

A newton is not very big. Hold a small apple, about 100 g, in your hand: the push your hand gives to hold it up on
Earth is about **1 newton**.

One kilogram is pulled down by gravity with a force of about **9.8 N** on Earth, and **3.7 N** on Mars. That pull is
the weight of the kilogram.

| Weight of | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A small apple (100 g) | 0.37 N | 0.98 N |
| A bag of sugar (1 kg) | 3.7 N | 9.8 N |
| A child (30 kg) | 111 N | 294 N |
| Perseverance (1,025 kg) | 3,800 N | 10,000 N |

A newton has an exact meaning: it is the force that makes a 1 kg mass speed up by 1 metre per second, every second,
with nothing else pushing or pulling. You meet that in level 5.

## Worked examples

**1. A 5 kg bag on Earth.** Each kilogram weighs about 9.8 N, so $5 \times 9.8 = 49$ N.

**2. The same bag on Mars.** $5 \times 3.7 = 18.5$ N. A bit more than a third.

**3. Lifting a suit pack.** A 40 kg backpack weighs $40 \times 9.8 = 392$ N on Earth, but $40 \times 3.7 = 148$ N on
Mars, about what 15 kg feels like on Earth.

## Try it

1. What is the unit of force?
2. About how much does a 100 g apple weigh on Earth?
3. Find the weight of a 2 kg rock on Mars.
4. Find the weight of the same rock on Earth.
5. An astronaut and suit have a mass of 150 kg. What is their weight on Mars?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The newton, N.
    2. About 1 N.
    3. $2 \times 3.7 = 7.4$ N.
    4. $2 \times 9.8 = 19.6$ N.
    5. $150 \times 3.7 = 555$ N.

## Common mistake

**"Weight is in kilograms."** In everyday talk we say so, but in physics, kilograms measure mass. Weight is a force,
measured in newtons, and it changes from planet to planet while the mass stays the same.
