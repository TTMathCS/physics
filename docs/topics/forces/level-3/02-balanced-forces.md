# Balanced forces

*Forces and motion · level 3, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Two teams pull on a rope in a tug of war, and the flag in the middle does not move. Is nobody pulling? Or is something
else going on?

## The idea

Forces on something are **balanced** when they cancel out: as much push one way as the other. In the tug of war, both
teams pull hard, but equally hard in opposite directions, so the rope stays put.

Balanced forces have a surprising result. They **do not change** how something moves:

- If it is **still**, it stays still. A book on a table.
- If it is **moving**, it keeps moving at the **same speed in the same direction**. A car cruising on a flat road,
  with the engine's push exactly balancing friction and the air.

That second point surprises most people. We expect that something moving needs a force to keep it going. But a force
is only needed to fight friction and air. In space, with nothing to fight, a spacecraft coasts for years with its
engines off.

**On Mars**, a parachute coming down at a steady speed has balanced forces too: the air pushing up on it as hard as
gravity pulls it down.

## Worked examples

**1. A hovering helicopter.** The rotors push air down, the air pushes the helicopter up, as hard as its weight
pulls down. Balanced: it stays in place. Ingenuity, the helicopter on Mars, hovered this way on its first flight.

**2. A falling skydiver at a steady speed.** Air resistance up equals weight down. Balanced: the speed stays the same.

## Try it

1. What does "balanced forces" mean?
2. A ball is lying still on the ground. Are the forces on it balanced?
3. A train moves at a steady speed along a straight track. Are the forces on it balanced?
4. A car's engine push is bigger than friction and air together. Are the forces balanced?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The forces cancel out: as much one way as the opposite way.
    2. Yes: its weight down and the ground's push up.
    3. Yes: steady speed in a straight line means balanced forces.
    4. No. The car will speed up.

## Common mistake

**"If something is moving, there must be an overall force pushing it forward."** Something moving at a steady speed in
a straight line has balanced forces. An overall force is needed only to change its speed or direction.
