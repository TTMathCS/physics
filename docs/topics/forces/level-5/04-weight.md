# Weight is a force, W = mg

*Forces and motion · level 5, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

Drop a hammer and a feather on the Moon, where there is no air. Which lands first? An astronaut tried it in 1971. What
happened, and what does it say about weight and mass?

## The idea

The **weight** of an object is the force of gravity on it:

$$W = m g,$$

where $g$ is the **gravitational field strength** at that place, in newtons per kilogram. It is the same number as the
free-fall acceleration in m/s², because $1 \text{ N/kg} = 1 \text{ m/s}^2$.

| | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $g$ at the surface | 3.71 N/kg | 9.81 N/kg |
| Weight of 1 kg | 3.71 N | 9.81 N |
| Weight of a 1,025 kg rover | 3,800 N | 10,060 N |
| Weight of an 80 kg person | 297 N | 785 N |

Now put weight into the second law for a thing falling with nothing else acting:

$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{mg}{m} = g.$$

The mass cancels. A heavy thing has a bigger pull, but also more inertia, by exactly the same factor. So **everything
falls with the same acceleration**, $g$, if air does not get in the way. On Apollo 15, David Scott dropped a hammer and a
feather together on the Moon. They landed at the same moment.

## Worked examples

**1. A sample bag.** A 2.5 kg bag of rocks on Mars: $W = 2.5 \times 3.71 = 9.3$ N. On Earth, $24.5$ N.

**2. The pull needed to lift it at a steady speed.** Balanced forces: the lift equals the weight, 9.3 N on Mars.

**3. Lifting it with an acceleration.** To lift the same bag upward at 1 m/s² on Mars:
$F - mg = ma$, so $F = m(g + a) = 2.5 \times 4.71 = 11.8$ N.

## Try it

1. Find the weight of a 50 kg crate on Mars and on Earth.
2. A spring scale on Mars reads 74.2 N for a box. What is its mass?
3. Why does a heavier thing not fall faster, if air does not get in the way?
4. What upward force lifts a 10 kg box at 0.5 m/s² upward acceleration on Mars?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Mars $50 \times 3.71 = 186$ N; Earth $50 \times 9.81 = 491$ N.
    2. $74.2 \div 3.71 = 20$ kg.
    3. Its bigger weight is matched by its bigger mass: $a = mg/m = g$ for every object.
    4. $F = 10 \times (3.71 + 0.5) = 42.1$ N.

## Common mistake

**"$g$ is the same everywhere because it is a constant."** Little $g$ changes from place to place: 9.81 on Earth, 3.71
on Mars, 1.62 on the Moon. It is big $G$, in Newton's law of gravity, that is the same everywhere.
