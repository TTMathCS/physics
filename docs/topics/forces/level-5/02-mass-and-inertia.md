# Mass and inertia

*Forces and motion · level 5, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

On the International Space Station, everything floats, and a bathroom scale reads zero. Yet doctors need to know if
astronauts are losing mass. How do you weigh someone who weighs nothing?

## The idea

**Mass** is the amount of matter in something, measured in kilograms. It is also a measure of **inertia**: how hard it
is to change the thing's velocity. Twice the mass needs twice the force to get the same change of speed in the same
time.

**Weight** is different. It is the force of gravity on the mass, and it depends on where you are. On Mars it is 0.38 of
Earth's; in orbit things are in free fall and feel weightless.

| A 70 kg astronaut | Mars surface | Earth surface | In orbit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mass | 70 kg | 70 kg | 70 kg |
| Weight felt | 260 N | 687 N | none (free fall) |
| Force to give 1 m/s² sideways | 70 N | 70 N | 70 N |

That last row is the key. Inertia does not depend on gravity, so it can be used to measure mass anywhere. On the space
station, astronauts strap into a device called SLAMMD, the Space Linear Acceleration Mass Measurement Device: a spring
pulls them along a short track, and the device times how fast they accelerate. From $m = F/a$ it works out their mass.

## Worked examples

**1. SLAMMD.** A spring pulls an astronaut with 140 N, and they accelerate at 2.0 m/s². Mass
$= 140 \div 2.0 = 70$ kg.

**2. A full and empty trolley on Mars.** The full one has three times the mass. With the same push it speeds up a
third as fast, exactly as on Earth.

**3. Shaking a box.** You can tell an empty box from a full one in space by shaking it: the full one fights back
harder. That is inertia, with no weight at all.

## Try it

1. What is the difference between mass and weight?
2. Does a rock have more inertia on Earth or on Mars?
3. A device pulls with 90 N and the astronaut accelerates at 1.5 m/s². What is the astronaut's mass?
4. Why can't a bathroom scale measure mass on the space station?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Mass is the amount of matter and measures inertia; weight is the force of gravity on it.
    2. The same: inertia depends only on mass.
    3. $90 \div 1.5 = 60$ kg.
    4. Everything there is in free fall: the person does not press on the scale, so it reads zero.

## Common mistake

**"Things are weightless in orbit because there is no gravity."** Gravity on the space station is about 90% of
Earth's. Everything there is falling round the Earth together, so nothing presses on anything.
