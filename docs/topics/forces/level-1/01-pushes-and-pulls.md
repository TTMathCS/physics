# Pushes and pulls

*Forces and motion · level 1, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

How do you open a door? How do you open a drawer? How do you get a friend going on a swing? Each time you do
something with your hands. What is it?

## The idea

Every time you move something, you **push** it or you **pull** it. You push a door shut. You pull a drawer open. You
push a swing. You pull a wagon by its handle.

A push or a pull is called a **force**. Forces are everywhere, even where you cannot see a hand:

- The ground pushes up on your feet, so you do not sink into it.
- A magnet pulls a paper clip.
- The Earth pulls everything down. That pull is called **gravity**.

A force always has two things in it: the thing that pushes or pulls, and the thing that is pushed or pulled. Your hand
pushes the door. The rope pulls the wagon.

**On Mars**, a rover moves because its wheels push back on the ground, and the ground pushes the rover forward.
Gravity on Mars pulls too, just not as hard as on Earth.

## Worked examples

**1. Kicking a ball.** Your foot pushes the ball. That is a push.

**2. Pulling on your socks.** Your hands pull the sock up your foot. That is a pull.

**3. A dog on a lead.** The dog pulls on the lead, and the lead pulls on your hand. Two pulls.

## Try it

1. Is opening a fridge door a push or a pull?
2. Is kicking a ball a push or a pull?
3. What pulls a dropped spoon down to the floor?
4. Name one push and one pull you did today.

??? note "Answers"
    1. A pull (and you push it shut again).
    2. A push.
    3. Gravity: the Earth's pull.
    4. Anything works, for example pushing a button and pulling a zip.

## Common mistake

**"Only people and animals make forces."** Lots of things push and pull without being alive: a magnet, the wind, the
ground under your feet, and the Earth's gravity.
