# Turning and squashing

*Forces and motion · level 1, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

A football is rolling towards you. You kick it from the side. Does it stop, or go faster, or does something else
happen? And what happens to a ball of play dough when you press it?

## The idea

A force can do more than start and stop things. It can also:

- **Change the way something is going.** Kick a rolling ball from the side, and it turns and goes a new way. A tennis
  racket sends the ball back the way it came.
- **Change its shape.** Press play dough, and it goes flat. Pull a rubber band, and it stretches. Squeeze a sponge,
  and it gets smaller.

Some things spring back when the force goes away, like a sponge or a rubber band. Others stay squashed, like play
dough or a crushed can.

**On Mars**, a rover has to steer round rocks. It turns by pointing its wheels a new way, so the push between the
wheels and the ground points along the new path.

## Worked examples

**1. Bouncing a ball.** When a ball hits the floor, the floor pushes it up. It changes the ball's way from down to up,
and for a moment it squashes the ball a little.

**2. A bow.** Pulling the string of a toy bow bends the bow. Let go, and it springs back, pushing the arrow off.

## Try it

1. Name the four things a force can do to something.
2. What happens to a rolling ball kicked from the side?
3. Name something that springs back after you squash it, and something that stays squashed.
4. When a ball bounces, what pushes it back up?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Start it, stop it, change the way it goes (or its speed), and change its shape.
    2. It turns and goes a new way.
    3. For example a sponge springs back; play dough stays squashed.
    4. The floor.

## Common mistake

**"Only a big force can turn something."** A small push from the side turns a moving thing a little; a bigger push
turns it more. Any sideways push turns it some.
