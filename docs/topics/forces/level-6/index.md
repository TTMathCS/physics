# Level 6: Friction and slopes

*Forces and motion · level 6 of 8 · rough age 13-14 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In this level friction gets numbers. You meet static and kinetic friction and the coefficient of friction, split the
weight of something on a ramp into two parts, and work out when it slides and how fast. Then come two surprises from
Mars: whether a parked rover slips on a slope does not depend on gravity at all, and nor, nearly, does the steepest
slope a sand dune can hold.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Static and kinetic friction](01-static-and-kinetic.md) | Explain the two kinds of friction and why it is harder to start a slide than to keep one going |
| [The coefficient of friction](02-coefficient-of-friction.md) | Use $F \le \mu N$ to find friction forces on Mars and on Earth |
| [Forces on a ramp](03-forces-on-a-ramp.md) | Split weight into parts along and across a slope |
| [Sliding down a ramp](04-sliding-down.md) | Find the acceleration of something sliding down a slope, with friction |
| [Parked on a Mars slope](05-parked-on-a-slope.md) | Show that the angle at which something slips does not depend on $g$, and find it |
| [Sand piles and dunes](06-sand-piles-and-dunes.md) | Explain the angle of repose and why Mars dunes are about as steep as Earth's |
