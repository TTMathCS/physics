# Turning needs a force

*Forces and motion · level 4, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

You swing a ball on a string round your head, then let go. Does it fly straight out from the middle, or off along the
circle, or somewhere else? And what was the string doing before you let go?

## The idea

Anything moving keeps going **in a straight line** unless a force acts on it. So to make something go round a curve,
something must keep pulling or pushing it **sideways, towards the inside of the curve**.

- The **string** pulls the swinging ball inwards.
- **Friction** between the tyres and the road pushes a turning car inwards.
- **Gravity** pulls the Moon towards the Earth and keeps it going round.

If that inward force stops, the thing carries straight on, along the direction it was going at that moment. The ball
you let go of flies off **along the circle's edge**, not straight outwards.

The faster you go round, or the tighter the curve, the bigger the inward force needed. That is why a car must slow down
for a sharp bend: the tyres can only grip so hard.

**On Mars**, gravity is weaker, so a vehicle presses less on the ground and its tyres grip less. It has to take bends
more slowly than it could on Earth. A Mars rover, at 4 cm/s, never has this problem.

## Worked examples

**1. Mud from a bike wheel.** Mud flies off a spinning wheel along the edge, at the point where it loses grip.

**2. A hammer thrower.** The athlete pulls the hammer inwards on its wire as it spins. On release, it flies off along
the line it was moving.

**3. A satellite.** If gravity could be switched off, a satellite in orbit would fly off in a straight line.

## Try it

1. What pulls a swinging ball inwards?
2. What makes a car able to go round a bend?
3. You let go of the string. Which way does the ball go?
4. Why do cars slow down for sharp bends?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The string.
    2. Friction between the tyres and the road, pushing inwards.
    3. Straight on, along the edge of the circle at that point.
    4. A sharp bend at high speed needs a bigger inward force than the tyres can grip.

## Common mistake

**"Something going round is thrown outwards by a force."** There is no outward force on the ball. It tries to go
straight; the string pulls it in. The "outward" feeling in a turning car is your body carrying on straight while the
car turns under you.
