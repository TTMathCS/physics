# Why rovers drive slowly

*Forces and motion · level 2, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

The rover Perseverance cost billions of dollars and has six motors in its wheels. Yet its top speed is slower than a
toddler crawling. Why don't they build it to go faster?

## The idea

| | Mars: Perseverance at top speed | Earth: a person walking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | about 150 m in an hour | about 5,000 m in an hour |
| Time to cross a football pitch | about 40 minutes | about 1 minute |

There are good reasons to go slowly:

- **Nobody can help.** If a rover tips over or gets stuck, there is no one on Mars to fix it. A slow rover has time to
  stop before trouble.
- **Rocks and sand.** The ground is full of sharp rocks and soft sand. Hitting a rock fast could break a wheel; at
  slow speed the rover just climbs over.
- **Messages take minutes.** A radio message takes between about 3 and 22 minutes to get from Earth to Mars. Nobody
  can steer a rover live like a toy car. The rover has to look, think and drive carefully on its own.
- **Little power.** The rover's power source gives a steady 110 watts or so, about as much as one bright old-style
  light bulb, topped up by batteries. The motors are geared down to turn slowly but strongly.

So the rovers spend most of their time standing still: taking pictures, studying rocks, and planning. On a good day,
Perseverance can drive itself a few hundred metres, using its cameras to pick a safe path as it goes.

## Worked examples

**1. A football pitch.** About 100 m long. At 150 m an hour, Perseverance would take about 40 minutes to drive it.

**2. Thinking as it drives.** Perseverance's computer can map the ground ahead and choose a path while it keeps
moving. Earlier rovers had to stop, look, think, and only then drive on.

## Try it

1. Give two reasons why rovers drive slowly.
2. About how long does a message take to get from Earth to Mars?
3. Why can't someone on Earth drive the rover live, like a toy car?
4. About how far can Perseverance drive in an hour at top speed?

??? note "Answers"
    1. For example: no one can fix it; rocks and sand; messages take minutes; little power.
    2. Between about 3 and 22 minutes, depending on where the planets are.
    3. By the time a message arrives, the rover would already have moved on. It has to drive itself carefully.
    4. About 150 m.

## Common mistake

**"Rovers are slow because Mars gravity makes them heavy."** Mars gravity is weaker than Earth's. Rovers are slow on
purpose, to be safe and careful far from any help.
