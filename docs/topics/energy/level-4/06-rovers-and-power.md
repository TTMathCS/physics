# Rovers and their power

*Energy and power · level 4, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

Perseverance drives, drills rocks, fires a laser, takes pictures and talks to Earth. A home on Earth runs a fridge,
lights and a TV. Which has more energy to spend each day?

## The idea

**Perseverance** gets its power from an **MMRTG**, a power source that turns the heat of decaying plutonium into
electricity. It gives a steady **110 W** or so, day and night. Two rechargeable batteries store energy so the rover can
use more than 110 W for short bursts, such as driving or drilling.

Over one sol, the MMRTG makes:

$$110 \text{ W} \times 24.66 \text{ h} = 2{,}700 \text{ Wh} = 2.7 \text{ kWh}.$$

| Energy for a day | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A machine | Perseverance: about 2.7 kWh a sol | A home in the United States: about 30 kWh |
| One adult's food | about 2.3 kWh | about 2.3 kWh |

The lander InSight, which ran on solar panels, got about 5 kWh a sol from them soon after it landed, before dust
settled on them.

So Perseverance, a car-sized laboratory, has about **a tenth** of the energy of an American home each day, and only a
little more than a person eats. Every activity is planned to fit.

The helicopter **Ingenuity** needed about 350 W while flying, more than three times the MMRTG's output, so it flew only
a minute or two at a time on its batteries, then spent the rest of the sol recharging from its small solar panel.

## Worked examples

**1. A drive.** Suppose driving uses about 200 W more than resting. An hour of driving: $0.2 \times 1 = 0.2$ kWh, about a
fourteenth of the sol's budget.

**2. Ingenuity's flight.** 350 W for 2 minutes (1/30 h): $350 \div 30 \approx 12$ Wh. Its panel could make that in a
few hours of sunlight.

## Try it

1. Perseverance's MMRTG gives 110 W. How much energy is that in a whole sol, in kWh?
2. Why does the rover need batteries, if the MMRTG never stops?
3. A science instrument uses 40 W for 3 hours. What share of a 2.7 kWh sol is that?
4. Why does a home on Earth have so much more energy to spend?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $110 \times 24.66 = 2{,}713$ Wh, about 2.7 kWh.
    2. Some jobs need more than 110 W at once; the batteries store energy for those bursts.
    3. $40 \times 3 = 120$ Wh, about 4.4% of 2,700 Wh.
    4. It is connected to a grid of big power stations.

## Common mistake

**"110 W is too little to do anything."** As a steady supply all day and night, 110 W adds up to 2.7 kWh a sol. With
batteries to save it up, that is enough for years of driving and science.
