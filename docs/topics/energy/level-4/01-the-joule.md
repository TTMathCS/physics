# The joule

*Energy and power · level 4, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

We measure distance in metres and mass in kilograms. What do we measure energy in, and how much is one unit? Is it the
same on Mars?

## The idea

Energy is measured in **joules**, written **J**, after James Prescott Joule, the English brewer and scientist who showed
in the 1840s that work and heat are two forms of the same thing.

**One joule** is the energy needed to push with a force of 1 newton over a distance of 1 metre. It is a small amount:

- Lifting a 100 g apple up by 1 m on Earth takes about **1 J**.
- A tennis serve gives the ball about 100 J.
- A slice of bread holds about 300,000 J.

Because the numbers get big, we often use **kilojoules** (1 kJ = 1,000 J) and **megajoules** (1 MJ = 1,000,000 J).

The joule is the same everywhere. But gravity is not, so one joule lifts things **higher** on Mars:

| 1 joule is enough to | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lift a 100 g apple by | 2.7 m | 1.0 m |
| Lift a 1 kg bag by | 27 cm | 10 cm |
| Lift a 50 kg person by | 5.4 mm | 2.0 mm |

## Worked examples

**1. Lifting a bag of rice.** A 1 kg bag weighs 3.71 N on Mars. Lifting it 1 m takes $3.71 \times 1 = 3.71$ J. On Earth,
$9.81$ J.

**2. Kilojoules.** 250,000 J is 250 kJ, or 0.25 MJ.

**3. Climbing a flight of stairs.** A 40 kg child climbs 3 m. On Earth: $40 \times 9.81 \times 3 = 1{,}177$ J, about
1.2 kJ. On Mars: $40 \times 3.71 \times 3 = 445$ J.

## Try it

1. What is the unit of energy?
2. How much energy does it take to lift a 1 kg bag by 1 m on Earth? On Mars?
3. Write 4,500 J in kJ.
4. A 60 kg astronaut climbs a 5 m ladder on Mars. How much energy does it take (ignoring waste)?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The joule, J.
    2. About 9.8 J on Earth, 3.7 J on Mars.
    3. 4.5 kJ.
    4. $60 \times 3.71 \times 5 = 1{,}113$ J, about 1.1 kJ.

## Common mistake

**"Energy is measured in watts."** Watts measure **power**, how fast energy is used. Energy is in joules (or
kilowatt-hours). That is the next lesson.
