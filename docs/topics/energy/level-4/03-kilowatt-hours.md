# Kilowatt-hours

*Energy and power · level 4, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

An electricity bill does not count joules. It counts "units", or kWh. What is a kWh, and how many joules is it?

## The idea

A joule is so small that a home uses millions of them every hour. So electricity is measured in a bigger unit, the
**kilowatt-hour**, kWh:

**1 kWh is the energy used by something of 1 kW running for 1 hour.**

$$1 \text{ kWh} = 1000 \text{ W} \times 3600 \text{ s} = 3{,}600{,}000 \text{ J} = 3.6 \text{ MJ}.$$

To find the energy in kWh: **kilowatts × hours**.

| Appliance | Power | Time | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LED lamp | 0.008 kW | 5 hours | 0.04 kWh |
| Kettle | 2 kW | 3 minutes (0.05 h) | 0.1 kWh |
| Oven | 2.5 kW | 1 hour | 2.5 kWh |
| Electric heater | 2 kW | 8 hours | 16 kWh |

All three bars are the same energy: 1 kWh. A home in the United States used on average about 30 kWh a day in 2022,
10,791 kWh in the year.

**On Mars**, a habitat's engineers would count energy in kWh too, and plan how many kWh the panels or power unit make in
a sol and how many the habitat needs.

## Worked examples

**1. kWh to joules.** 2.5 kWh $= 2.5 \times 3.6 = 9$ MJ.

**2. A laptop.** 50 W is 0.05 kW. Four hours: $0.05 \times 4 = 0.2$ kWh.

**3. A bill.** At 15 cents a kWh, the heater above for 8 hours costs $16 \times 15 = 240$ cents.

## Try it

1. How many joules are in 1 kWh?
2. A 3 kW oven runs for 40 minutes. How many kWh does it use?
3. Change 18 MJ into kWh.
4. A habitat's panels make 50 kWh in a sol. How many hours could they run a 2 kW heater?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 3,600,000 J (3.6 MJ).
    2. $3 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 2$ kWh.
    3. $18 \div 3.6 = 5$ kWh.
    4. $50 \div 2 = 25$ hours.

## Common mistake

**"kWh means kilowatts per hour."** It is kilowatts **times** hours: a power multiplied by a time, which gives an energy.
