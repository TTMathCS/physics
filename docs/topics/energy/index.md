# Energy and power

A city on Mars runs on energy: to heat its rooms, grow its food, split water and run its machines. This topic
starts with what makes things go and climbs to work, power, efficiency and the real choices for Mars: sunlight that
is less than half as strong as at Earth, dust on the panels, batteries, and small nuclear reactors.

8 levels. The ages are only a rough guide for parents and teachers; anyone can start at any level.

| Level | Rough age | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1: What makes things go | 5-6 | Food, sunlight, batteries and wind; energy is used to do things |
| Level 2: Stored and moving | 7-8 | Stretched springs, raised weights, moving things; energy changes form |
| Level 3: Energy in, energy out | 9-10 | Nothing is lost, only changed; where wasted energy goes |
| Level 4: Joules and watts | 11-12 | Measuring energy and power, kilowatt-hours, the power a person uses |
| Level 5: Work and kinetic energy | 12-13 | W = Fd, ½mv², mgh, a dropped tool on Mars and on Earth |
| Level 6: Efficiency and the Sun | 13-14 | Efficiency, solar power per square metre at Mars (about 590 W/m², Earth's 1361), panels and dust |
| Level 7: Storing energy | 15-16 | Batteries, hydrogen, flywheels, pumped storage; getting through a dust storm |
| Level 8: Power for a city | 17 and beyond | Nuclear fission and radioisotope power, a full energy budget for Arcadia City |
