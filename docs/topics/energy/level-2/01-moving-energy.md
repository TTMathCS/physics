# Moving energy

*Energy and power · level 2, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A ping-pong ball rolls into a tower of blocks, and nothing happens. A bowling ball rolls into it at the same speed,
and the tower crashes down. Why can one moving ball do so much more than the other?

## The idea

Everything that moves has energy, just because it is moving. This is called **movement energy**, or by its proper name,
**kinetic energy**.

A moving thing has **more** kinetic energy if it is:

- **heavier**: a bowling ball has far more than a ping-pong ball at the same speed;
- **faster**: a ball thrown hard has more than the same ball rolled gently. Speed matters a lot: twice as fast means
  **four times** the energy.

When a moving thing hits something, it can pass its energy on: knocking blocks over, making a dent, making a bang, or
warming things up a little.

**On Mars**, a ball rolling at a certain speed has exactly the same kinetic energy as on Earth. Moving energy depends
on how heavy something is and how fast it goes, not on gravity. So a cart rolling into you on Mars hits just as hard as
it would on Earth.

## Worked examples

**1. A car and a bike.** At the same speed, a car has far more kinetic energy than a bike, because it is much heavier.
That is why it needs much bigger brakes.

**2. Twice as fast.** A ball rolling twice as fast has four times as much energy. It can knock over four times as many
blocks, or roll four times as far up a slope.

## Try it

1. What do we call the energy of a moving thing?
2. Which has more kinetic energy: a lorry or a car, at the same speed?
3. A ball goes twice as fast. How many times more energy does it have?
4. Does a rolling cart have less kinetic energy on Mars than on Earth, at the same speed?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Kinetic energy (movement energy).
    2. The lorry: it is heavier.
    3. Four times.
    4. No, the same: kinetic energy does not depend on gravity.

## Common mistake

**"Twice as fast means twice the energy."** Kinetic energy grows faster than speed: twice the speed gives four times the
energy, and three times the speed gives nine times.
