# Level 8: Power for a city

*Energy and power · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

The last level goes to the nucleus. You see how a rover's radioisotope generator works and why it fades, how fission
gets so much energy out of so little fuel, and what a reactor on Mars must do with its waste heat. Then you meet the
limit on every heat engine, build an energy budget for a city of a thousand people, and choose how to power it.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Radioisotope power](01-radioisotope-power.md) | Explain how an MMRTG makes electricity, and use half-life to find its power years later |
| [Fission](02-fission.md) | Find the energy released by splitting uranium, and link it to $E = mc^2$ |
| [A reactor for Mars](03-a-reactor-for-mars.md) | Size a reactor's radiator, and use the inverse square law to place it |
| [Heat engines](04-heat-engines.md) | Use the Carnot limit, $1 - T_c/T_h$, on Mars and on Earth |
| [A city energy budget](05-a-city-energy-budget.md) | Build an energy budget for a Mars city and size its power supply |
| [Choosing power](06-choosing-power.md) | Weigh solar, storage and nuclear power, and argue for a mix |
