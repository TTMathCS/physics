# A dropped tool

*Energy and power · level 5, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

An astronaut on a 10 m scaffold drops a 0.5 kg spanner. How fast is it going when it hits the ground on Mars? On Earth?
And how much energy does it hit with?

## The idea

As the spanner falls, its potential energy turns into kinetic energy. With almost no air (as on Mars), almost none is
lost:

$$mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad v = \sqrt{2gh}.$$

The mass cancels, so all dropped things land at the same speed from the same height. But the energy they hit with,
$mgh$, does depend on mass.

| Dropped from 10 m | Mars | Earth (no air) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed at the ground, $\sqrt{2gh}$ | 8.6 m/s, 31 km/h | 14.0 m/s, 50 km/h |
| Energy of a 0.5 kg spanner | 18.6 J | 49.1 J |
| Time to fall, $\sqrt{2h/g}$ | 2.3 s | 1.4 s |
| Height on Earth that gives the same landing speed | 3.8 m | 10 m |

So a fall on Mars from 10 m is like a fall on Earth from 3.8 m. Still dangerous, but much less so. Helmets, toe caps and
tool tethers would still be needed on any building site on Mars.

## Worked examples

**1. Speed on Mars.** $v = \sqrt{2 \times 3.71 \times 10} = \sqrt{74.2} = 8.6$ m/s.

**2. The same speed on Earth.** $h = v^2/(2g) = 74.2/19.62 = 3.8$ m.

**3. A person jumping down.** From 1 m: on Mars $\sqrt{7.42} = 2.7$ m/s, on Earth $\sqrt{19.6} = 4.4$ m/s. Landing from
a jump of 2.6 m on Mars feels like landing from 1 m on Earth.

## Try it

1. A rock falls 5 m on Mars. Find its landing speed.
2. From what height on Earth does a rock land at the same speed?
3. A 2 kg tool falls 6 m on Mars. With what kinetic energy does it land?
4. Why does the landing speed not depend on the mass?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $\sqrt{2 \times 3.71 \times 5} = \sqrt{37.1} = 6.1$ m/s.
    2. $37.1 / 19.62 = 1.9$ m.
    3. $2 \times 3.71 \times 6 = 44.5$ J.
    4. Both $mgh$ and $\tfrac12 mv^2$ are proportional to $m$, so it cancels.

## Common mistake

**"Weaker gravity means the speed is 0.38 times as much."** The landing speed is $\sqrt{2gh}$, so it goes as
$\sqrt{g}$: $\sqrt{0.38} = 0.62$ of Earth's. The energy is 0.38 of Earth's.
