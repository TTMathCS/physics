# Gravitational potential energy

*Energy and power · level 5, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A water tank sits on a tower 20 m above a Mars habitat. On Earth, a tank that high gives good water pressure for free.
Does it store as much energy on Mars?

## The idea

Lift a mass $m$ by a height $h$ at a steady speed. The force needed equals its weight $mg$, and it moves a distance
$h$, so the work done is $mgh$. That work is stored as **gravitational potential energy**:

$$E_p = mgh.$$

It is "potential" because it can come back: let the mass fall, and the stored energy turns into kinetic energy.

Only **changes** in height matter. You can measure $h$ from any level you like, the floor, the ground or the bottom of a
crater, as long as you are consistent.

| Lifting 1,000 kg (a cubic metre of water) by 20 m | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $g$ | 3.71 N/kg | 9.81 N/kg |
| Energy stored, $mgh$ | 74 kJ | 196 kJ |
| Water pressure at the bottom of the pipe, $\rho g h$ | 74 kPa | 196 kPa |

So on Mars, the tank stores 0.38 as much energy and gives 0.38 of the pressure. To get Earth's pressure from a tank,
you would need a tower 2.6 times as tall, or a pump.

The formula $mgh$ is only right where $g$ is about the same over the whole height: near a planet's surface. For rockets
and orbits, gravity weakens with height, and a different formula is needed.

## Worked examples

**1. The water tank.** $1000 \times 3.71 \times 20 = 74{,}200$ J on Mars; $196{,}200$ J on Earth.

**2. A climber on Olympus Mons.** The volcano's summit is about 22 km above the Mars datum, the planet's "sea level".
Climbing from the datum with a 150 kg suit and pack: $150 \times 3.71 \times 22{,}000 = 1.22\times10^7$ J, about
3.4 kWh. (Gravity is nearly the same at the top: 22 km is small compared with the radius of Mars.)

**3. Choosing the zero.** A book on a shelf 2 m up in a room on Earth, the floor 3 m above the ground. Relative to the
floor: $1 \times 9.81 \times 2 = 19.6$ J. Relative to the ground: 49 J. Only differences matter.

## Try it

1. Find the potential energy gained by a 5 kg rock lifted 2 m on Mars.
2. How much energy is stored lifting a 300 kg module by 12 m on Earth?
3. A pump lifts 2,000 kg of water up 15 m on Mars. What energy does it give the water?
4. Why can't $mgh$ be used for a rocket climbing to 1,000 km?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $5 \times 3.71 \times 2 = 37.1$ J.
    2. $300 \times 9.81 \times 12 = 35{,}300$ J.
    3. $2000 \times 3.71 \times 15 = 111{,}300$ J.
    4. Gravity is noticeably weaker that high, so $g$ is not constant over the climb.

## Common mistake

**Using the length of the path instead of the height.** Walking 100 m up a gentle ramp that rises 5 m gains $mg \times 5$,
not $mg \times 100$. Only the change in height counts.
