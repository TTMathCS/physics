# Speeding up

*Rockets and landing · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

At lift-off a big rocket rises slowly, as if it were struggling. Eight minutes later it is going 7,800 m every second.
How does it speed up so much, and why does it get faster more and more quickly?

## The idea

How quickly something gains speed is its **acceleration**, measured in metres per second **every second** (m/s²). It
depends on two things:

- the **net force** on it (the leftover push);
- its **mass** (how much stuff there is to move).

$$\text{acceleration} = \frac{\text{net force}}{\text{mass}}$$

A bigger push speeds it up faster; a bigger mass speeds up more slowly.

**At lift-off,** the Saturn V's net force was about 5,000 kN on a mass of about 2,970 tonnes (2,970,000 kg).
Acceleration $= 5{,}000{,}000 \div 2{,}970{,}000 \approx 1.7$ m/s²: slow. After one second it was going only 1.7 m/s, a
gentle walk.

**As it climbs,** it burns propellant at about 13 tonnes **every second**. Its mass drops fast, but the engines push as
hard as ever (in fact a little harder, as the air thins). So the acceleration grows. Near the end of the first stage's
burn, the Saturn V was speeding up at about 4 g, nearly 40 m/s every second, and the astronauts were pressed into their
seats by four times their weight.

| Saturn V, first stage | At lift-off | Near the end of the burn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mass | about 2,970 t | about 800 t |
| Engines firing | 5 | 4 |
| Thrust | about 34,000 kN | about 31,000 kN |
| Acceleration | about 1.7 m/s² | about 38 m/s² (about 4 g) |

(The centre engine was shut down early, so the acceleration would not climb past about 4 g.)

## Worked examples

**1. A light model rocket.** Mass 0.1 kg, net force 5 N. Acceleration $= 5 \div 0.1 = 50$ m/s²: after 1 second it is
going 50 m/s. Small rockets leap; big ones rise.

**2. Half the mass.** If a rocket burns half its mass and the net force stays the same, its acceleration doubles.

## Try it

1. A rocket has a net force of 2,000 kN and a mass of 1,000,000 kg. Find its acceleration.
2. If the same rocket's mass falls to 500,000 kg with the same net force, what is its acceleration?
3. Why does a big rocket speed up faster and faster during its burn?
4. A rocket has an acceleration of 20 m/s². How fast is it going after 5 seconds, starting from rest?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $2{,}000{,}000 \div 1{,}000{,}000 = 2$ m/s².
    2. 4 m/s².
    3. It burns propellant and gets lighter, but the thrust stays the same (or grows), so each second it speeds up more.
    4. $20 \times 5 = 100$ m/s.

## Common mistake

**Using the thrust instead of the net force.** At lift-off most of the thrust only holds the rocket up against its
weight. Only the leftover force speeds it up.
