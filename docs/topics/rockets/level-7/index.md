# Level 7: Landing on Mars

*Rockets and landing · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

This level works out the landing on Mars from the physics up. You derive the drag equation and the ballistic
coefficient, model the thin Martian air with height, find a parachute's terminal speed, estimate the heating on a heat
shield, see why heavy landers must fire their engines while still supersonic, and finish with the first powered flight
on another world.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The drag equation](01-the-drag-equation.md) | Derive $F = \tfrac12\rho v^2 C_d A$ from momentum, and use the ballistic coefficient |
| [Thin air](02-thin-air.md) | Model Mars's air with $\rho = \rho_0 e^{-h/H}$, find Mach numbers, and compare with Earth |
| [Parachutes and terminal speed](03-parachute-terminal-speed.md) | Find terminal speeds under a parachute on Mars, and size a parachute for a given landing speed |
| [Entry heating](04-entry-heating.md) | Estimate heating with $q \propto \sqrt{\rho/r_n}\,v^3$, and explain blunt shapes and ablation |
| [Supersonic retropropulsion](05-supersonic-retropropulsion.md) | Explain why heavy landers stay supersonic near the ground, and must brake with engines |
| [Flying on Mars](06-flying-on-mars.md) | Work out the lift and hover power of a helicopter in Mars air, from the numbers of Ingenuity |
