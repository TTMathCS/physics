# Level 6: The rocket equation

*Rockets and landing · level 6 of 8 · rough age 13-14 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

This level reaches the most important equation in spaceflight. You start from conservation of momentum, work out recoil,
measure engines by specific impulse, define a rocket's mass ratio, and then derive Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation,
$\Delta v = v_e \ln(m_0/m_1)$. It explains why rockets are mostly propellant, why getting off Earth is so hard, and why
rockets come in stages.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Conservation of momentum](01-conservation-of-momentum.md) | Use the conservation of momentum for collisions, pushes and dockings |
| [Recoil](02-recoil.md) | Work out recoil speeds, and see why a rocket's gain shrinks with each kilogram it throws |
| [Specific impulse](03-specific-impulse.md) | Convert between specific impulse and exhaust speed, and compare propellants |
| [Mass ratio](04-mass-ratio.md) | Find a rocket's mass ratio and propellant fraction, and say what limits them |
| [Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation](05-the-rocket-equation.md) | Derive and use $\Delta v = v_e \ln(m_0/m_1)$ for launches, landings and the trip to Mars |
| [Staging, worked out](06-staging.md) | Compare one stage with two and three, and find the payload each can carry |
