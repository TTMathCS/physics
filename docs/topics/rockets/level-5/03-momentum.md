# Momentum

*Rockets and landing · level 5, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

The Perseverance rover drives at about 4 cm per second. A rifle bullet flies at about 900 m/s. Which is harder to stop?

## The idea

How hard something is to stop depends on both its mass and its speed. Newton called the product the **quantity of
motion**; we call it **momentum**:

$$p = m v.$$

Its unit is kg·m/s. Momentum has a direction, like velocity: a rover going east and one going west have opposite momenta.

| | Mass | Speed | Momentum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Perseverance, at top speed | 1,025 kg | 0.042 m/s | 43 kg·m/s |
| A rifle bullet | 0.01 kg | 900 m/s | 9 kg·m/s |
| A 70 kg person, walking | 70 kg | 1.4 m/s | 98 kg·m/s |
| A car on the motorway | 1,500 kg | 30 m/s | 45,000 kg·m/s |
| A lander under its parachute | 2,000 kg | 90 m/s | 180,000 kg·m/s |
| Saturn V exhaust, each second | 13,000 kg | about 2,600 m/s | about 34,000,000 kg·m/s |

The slow rover has nearly five times the momentum of the fast bullet.

**Newton's second law, the momentum way.** Newton actually wrote his second law as: force equals the **rate of change of
momentum**,

$$F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}.$$

When the mass stays the same, $\Delta p = m\Delta v$, and this is $F = ma$ again. But it also works when mass changes,
as it does for a rocket throwing out exhaust. The last row of the table shows why: each second the Saturn V's engines
gave about 34 million kg·m/s of momentum to their exhaust, which is a force of 34 MN, the thrust.

## Worked examples

**1. The rover.** $p = 1025 \times 0.042 = 43$ kg·m/s.

**2. Stopping a car.** To stop a 45,000 kg·m/s car in 5 s, the brakes need $F = 45{,}000/5 = 9{,}000$ N.

**3. The exhaust.** An engine throws out 300 kg of gas each second at 2,800 m/s. Momentum given to the gas per second:
$300 \times 2800 = 840{,}000$ kg·m/s, so the thrust is 840 kN. (That is about one Merlin engine.)

## Try it

1. Find the momentum of a 0.5 kg ball thrown at 20 m/s.
2. A 2,000 kg lander falls at 90 m/s. A 10,000 kg one falls at 18 m/s. Which has more momentum?
3. What force stops a 1,500 kg car from 20 m/s in 4 s?
4. An engine throws out 100 kg of gas each second at 3,500 m/s. What is its thrust?
5. Why is momentum more useful than speed alone for describing a crash?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $10$ kg·m/s.
    2. They are equal: $180{,}000$ kg·m/s each.
    3. $\Delta p = 30{,}000$; $F = 7{,}500$ N.
    4. $350{,}000$ N, or 350 kN.
    5. It includes the mass: a slow, heavy thing can be just as hard to stop as a fast, light one.

## Common mistake

**Thinking momentum and energy are the same.** Momentum is $mv$ and has a direction; kinetic energy is $\tfrac12mv^2$ and
does not. A bullet and a rover with the same momentum have very different energies: the bullet's is far larger.
