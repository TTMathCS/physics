# Mixing

*Radiation and the atom · level 1, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Stir some raisins into a bowl of cereal. Is it a new kind of food, or still cereal and raisins?

## The idea

When you stir different things together, you make a **mixture**. The things in a mixture are still there, each one
still itself. They are just jumbled up.

- Cereal and raisins: you can still see and pick out both.
- Sand and pebbles: still sand, still pebbles.
- Air: a mixture of gases you cannot see, mostly nitrogen and oxygen.

Some mixtures are easy to see, like a fruit salad. Others look like one thing, like air or tap water, but are mixtures
too.

## Worked examples

**1. A fruit salad.** Apple, banana and grapes, all mixed. Each piece is still apple, banana or grape.

**2. Sand and salt.** Stir them together. It looks like one powder, but it is still sand and salt.

**3. Air.** You breathe a mixture every moment. Most of it is nitrogen, and about a fifth is oxygen.

## Try it

1. What is a mixture?
2. In a bowl of cereal and raisins, are the raisins still raisins?
3. Name a mixture you can see the parts of.
4. Name a mixture you cannot see the parts of.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Different things stirred together, each still itself.
    2. Yes.
    3. Any one: a fruit salad, cereal and raisins, sand and pebbles.
    4. Any one: air, tap water, sea water.

## Common mistake

**"Mixing makes a new thing."** In a mixture, nothing new is made. Everything that went in is still there, and it can be
sorted out again.
