# Mass per area

*Radiation and the atom · level 7, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

Lead halves a 1 MeV gamma beam in 0.86 cm; water takes 9.8 cm. So is lead eleven times better? Or is something else
going on?

## The idea

Look at the column $\mu/\rho$ in the last lesson: about 0.07 cm²/g for lead, concrete and water alike. For 1 MeV gamma
rays, a gram of lead stops about as much as a gram of water. Lead is thinner only because it packs more grams into each
centimetre.

So shielders measure a shield by its **mass per area**: the grams of material over each square centimetre,

$$\text{mass per area (g/cm}^2) = \text{density (g/cm}^3) \times \text{thickness (cm)}.$$

At 1 MeV, every one of these materials halves the beam in about **10 g/cm²**. For cosmic rays it is not a simple
halving, but mass per area is still the measure that counts, and it is what a rocket must launch.

The air is a shield too. The pressure at the ground is the weight of the air above each square metre:

| Air above you | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mass over each m² | about 171 kg | about 10,300 kg |
| Mass per area | about 17 g/cm² | about 1,030 g/cm² |
| The same as a layer of water | 17 cm deep | about 10 m deep |

## Worked examples

**1. Mars's air.** The surface pressure is 636 Pa and gravity 3.73 m/s². Mass over each m² $= 636 / 3.73 \approx 171$
kg. That is $171{,}000$ g over $10{,}000$ cm²: 17 g/cm².

**2. Earth's air.** $101{,}400 / 9.82 \approx 10{,}300$ kg over each m², or 1,030 g/cm². Water is 1 g/cm³, so that is
the same as 1,030 cm, about 10 m, of water.

**3. Earth's air against a lead wall.** 1,030 g/cm² of lead is $1{,}030 / 11.35 \approx 91$ cm thick. Everyone on
Earth lives under as much mass as nearly a metre of lead, spread out as air.

## Try it

1. Find the mass per area of a 20 cm concrete wall (2.3 g/cm³).
2. How thick a layer of water has the same mass per area as Mars's air?
3. Why does a gram of lead stop about as much 1 MeV gamma radiation as a gram of water?
4. Earth's air gives about how many times the mass per area of Mars's air?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $2.3 \times 20 = 46$ g/cm².
    2. About 17 cm.
    3. The coefficient $\mu/\rho$ is nearly the same, about 0.07 cm²/g, for both.
    4. $1{,}030 / 17 \approx 60$ times.

## Common mistake

**Comparing shields by thickness alone.** A thin lead sheet and a thick water tank can give the same protection. Compare
the grams over each square centimetre.
