# Regolith shields

*Radiation and the atom · level 7, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Every kilogram of shielding launched from Earth costs a great deal. But on Mars there is a shield lying everywhere,
free: the ground. How much of it would a home need?

## The idea

Mars's loose surface soil and broken rock is called **regolith**. Piled over a home, it adds mass per area.

Suppose loose regolith has a density of about 1.5 g/cm³. Then each metre of it adds $1.5 \times 100 = 150$ g/cm²,
almost nine times all of Mars's air.

But a thin layer can make things **worse**. Cosmic rays smashing nuclei in the soil make secondary neutrons. A 2022
study using Curiosity's measurements found that the dose **peaks about 30 cm down**, higher than at the surface. Only
deeper does it fall. The study found that **1 to 1.6 m** of regolith would keep a year's dose under 100 mSv.

| Shielding above each cm² | Mars, under 1 m of regolith | Earth, in the open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air | 17 g | 1,030 g |
| Regolith (supposing 1.5 g/cm³) | 150 g | none |
| Total | about 170 g | about 1,030 g |

## Worked examples

**1. One metre.** $1.5 \times 100 = 150$ g/cm². With the air, about 170 g/cm²: still only a sixth of Earth's air.

**2. A roof.** Suppose a habitat roof of 10 m by 10 m is covered with 1.5 m of regolith. Volume $= 100 \times 1.5 = 150$
m³. At 1,500 kg/m³, that is 225,000 kg, 225 tonnes of soil, to be dug and moved by machine.

**3. As much as Earth's air.** To match Earth's 1,030 g/cm² you would need $(1{,}030 - 17) / 1.5 \approx 675$ cm of
regolith: nearly 7 m.

## Try it

1. What is regolith?
2. Why can 30 cm of regolith give a higher dose than none?
3. Find the mass per area of 2 m of regolith at 1.5 g/cm³.
4. Why is regolith a cheaper shield than water brought from Earth?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The loose soil and broken rock on the surface.
    2. Cosmic rays smash nuclei in the soil and make secondary neutrons; too thin a layer adds them without blocking
       enough.
    3. $1.5 \times 200 = 300$ g/cm².
    4. It is already there; it does not have to be launched.

## Common mistake

**"Any cover is better than none."** A thin layer of soil makes extra secondary radiation. A regolith shield must be
thick enough, around a metre or more, to help.
