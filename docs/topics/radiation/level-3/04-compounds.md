# Compounds

*Radiation and the atom · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

Hydrogen is a gas that burns. Oxygen is a gas that helps things burn. Join them, and you get water, which puts fires out.
How can that be?

## The idea

When atoms of different elements **join** together, they make a **compound**. A compound is a new substance, often
nothing like the elements that made it.

A group of atoms joined together is a **molecule**. Its **formula** says which atoms it has, and how many:

| Compound | Formula | Made of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water | H₂O | 2 hydrogen atoms, 1 oxygen atom |
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ | 1 carbon atom, 2 oxygen atoms |
| Table salt | NaCl | sodium and chlorine, one of each |
| Rust | Fe₂O₃ | iron and oxygen, 2 to 3 |

Unlike a mixture, a compound cannot be pulled apart with a sieve or a magnet. It takes a chemical reaction.

## Worked examples

**1. Counting atoms.** CO₂ has 1 carbon and 2 oxygen atoms: 3 atoms in each molecule.

**2. Salt.** Sodium is a soft metal that fizzes in water; chlorine is a poisonous gas. Joined, they make table salt.

**3. Rust.** Iron joins with oxygen from the air, helped by water, to make rust: a new red-brown compound.

## Try it

1. What is a compound?
2. How many atoms are in one molecule of water?
3. What elements are in carbon dioxide?
4. Can a magnet pull the iron out of rust?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A substance made of atoms of different elements joined together.
    2. Three: two hydrogen and one oxygen.
    3. Carbon and oxygen.
    4. Not in the way it pulls iron filings: the iron is joined to oxygen in a new compound.

## Common mistake

**"A compound is a kind of mixture."** In a mixture, the parts are just jumbled. In a compound, the atoms are joined, and
the new substance behaves differently.
