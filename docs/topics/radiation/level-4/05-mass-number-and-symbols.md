# Mass number and symbols

*Radiation and the atom · level 4, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Scientists write uranium-238 as $^{238}_{92}\text{U}$. What do the two numbers tell you?

## The idea

Two numbers describe a nucleus:

- The **atomic number** $Z$: the number of protons. It decides the element.
- The **mass number** $A$: the number of protons plus neutrons.

So the number of neutrons is $N = A - Z$.

A **nuclear symbol** writes the mass number at the top left and the atomic number at the bottom left: $^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$,
where X is the element's symbol. Because protons and neutrons each weigh about 1 atomic mass unit (u), the mass number
is close to the atom's mass in u.

| Isotope | Symbol | Protons | Neutrons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon-14 | $^{14}_{6}\text{C}$ | 6 | 8 |
| Potassium-40 | $^{40}_{19}\text{K}$ | 19 | 21 |
| Argon-40 | $^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ | 18 | 22 |
| Uranium-238 | $^{238}_{92}\text{U}$ | 92 | 146 |

## Worked examples

**1. Uranium-238.** $Z = 92$, $A = 238$, so $N = 238 - 92 = 146$ neutrons.

**2. Same mass number, different element.** Potassium-40 and argon-40 both have $A = 40$. Potassium has 19 protons and
argon 18. When a potassium-40 nucleus breaks down, it can turn into argon-40. That is how rocks are dated (level 8).

**3. Argon on Earth.** NIST gives Earth's argon as 99.60% argon-40, 0.33% argon-36 and 0.06% argon-38. Most of it is
argon-40, made over billions of years by potassium-40 breaking down in rocks.

## Try it

1. What is the mass number?
2. How many neutrons are in $^{56}_{26}\text{Fe}$?
3. Write the symbol for oxygen-16 (oxygen has 8 protons).
4. Argon-36 and argon-40: which has more neutrons, and by how many?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The number of protons plus neutrons in the nucleus.
    2. $56 - 26 = 30$.
    3. $^{16}_{8}\text{O}$.
    4. Argon-40, by 4 (22 neutrons against 18).

## Common mistake

**Subtracting the wrong way.** The neutrons are $A - Z$, the big number minus the small one. $Z - A$ would give a
negative number of neutrons.
