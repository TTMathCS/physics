# Electrons

*Radiation and the atom · level 4, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

A balloon rubbed on your hair picks up a charge. Where does the charge come from?

## The idea

**Electrons** are the light, negatively charged particles around the nucleus. J. J. Thomson found them in 1897. An
electron is about 1,836 times lighter than a proton, but its charge is just as big, the other way round.

- A whole atom has as many electrons as protons, so the charges cancel and the atom is **neutral**.
- If an atom loses an electron, it is left **positive**. If it gains one, it becomes **negative**. A charged atom is an
  **ion**.
- In a simple picture, electrons sit in **shells** around the nucleus. The first holds 2, the next 8, and the third
  holds 8 for the first twenty elements. (Really, electrons are spread out in clouds, not tiny dots on rings.)

Rubbing a balloon moves electrons from your hair to the balloon: that is static electricity. Knocking electrons out of
atoms is also what makes some radiation harmful, as the next level shows.

## Worked examples

**1. Counting electrons.** Sodium has 11 protons, so a neutral sodium atom has 11 electrons: 2, then 8, then 1.

**2. An ion.** Sodium easily loses that one outer electron. Then it has 11 protons and 10 electrons: a charge of +1.

**3. The mass.** An electron is $9.11 \times 10^{-31}$ kg; a proton $1.67 \times 10^{-27}$ kg.
$1.67 \times 10^{-27} \div 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \approx 1{,}836$.

## Try it

1. What charge does an electron have?
2. Why is a whole atom neutral?
3. Oxygen has 8 electrons. How many are in each shell?
4. A chlorine atom (17 protons) gains an electron. What is its charge now?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Negative.
    2. It has as many electrons as protons, and their charges cancel.
    3. 2 in the first shell, 6 in the second.
    4. 18 electrons and 17 protons: a charge of −1.

## Common mistake

**"An ion has a different number of protons."** An ion has gained or lost electrons. Its protons, and so its element,
stay the same.
