# Isotopes

*Radiation and the atom · level 4, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

All carbon atoms have 6 protons. Do they all have the same number of neutrons too?

## The idea

No. Atoms of the same element can have **different numbers of neutrons**. These different kinds are called **isotopes**
of the element.

Isotopes are named by their total number of protons and neutrons:

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons | Share of the carbon on Earth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon-12 | 6 | 6 | 98.93% |
| Carbon-13 | 6 | 7 | 1.07% |
| Carbon-14 | 6 | 8 | a tiny trace |

Hydrogen has three: ordinary hydrogen (no neutron), **deuterium** (one neutron) and **tritium** (two neutrons).

Isotopes of an element have the same electrons, so they behave the same way in chemistry. But the extra neutrons make
them heavier, and some isotopes, like carbon-14 and tritium, are **unstable**: their nuclei break down over time. They
are **radioactive**.

## Worked examples

**1. Counting neutrons.** Carbon-14 has 14 particles in its nucleus. 6 are protons, so $14 - 6 = 8$ are neutrons.

**2. Heavy water.** Water made with deuterium instead of ordinary hydrogen is called **heavy water**. A litre of it is
about 10% heavier than a litre of ordinary water.

**3. How rare.** Only about 1 hydrogen atom in 8,700 on Earth is deuterium (NIST gives 0.0115%):
$100 \div 0.0115 \approx 8{,}700$.

## Try it

1. What is an isotope?
2. How many neutrons does carbon-13 have?
3. Why do isotopes of an element behave the same way in chemistry?
4. What does it mean that carbon-14 is radioactive?

??? note "Answers"
    1. An atom of an element with a particular number of neutrons.
    2. $13 - 6 = 7$.
    3. They have the same number of electrons, which decide how atoms join.
    4. Its nucleus is unstable and breaks down over time.

## Common mistake

**"Isotopes are different elements."** Isotopes of carbon are all carbon, because they all have 6 protons. Only the
number of neutrons differs.
