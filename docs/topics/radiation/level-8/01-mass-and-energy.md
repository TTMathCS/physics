# Mass and energy

*Radiation and the atom · level 8, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Curiosity's nuclear battery gives out about 2,000 W of heat, year after year, with nothing added. Its plutonium gets
very slightly lighter as it does. By how much?

## The idea

In 1905, Albert Einstein showed that mass and energy are two forms of the same thing. Energy $E$ and mass $m$ are linked
by

$$E = mc^2,$$

where $c = 2.998 \times 10^8$ m/s is the speed of light. Since $c^2$ is huge, a tiny mass is a vast energy: 1 gram is
$9.0 \times 10^{13}$ J.

Any system that gives out energy loses mass, $m = E/c^2$. In chemistry the loss is far too small to measure. In
nuclear reactions it is large enough to weigh.

Nuclear physicists measure mass in **atomic mass units** (u) and energy in **mega-electronvolts** (MeV;
1 MeV $= 1.602 \times 10^{-13}$ J). By $E = mc^2$, NIST gives

$$1 \text{ u} = 931.494 \text{ MeV}.$$

## Worked examples

**1. One gram.** $E = 0.001 \times (2.998\times10^8)^2 = 9.0\times10^{13}$ J: about what a large power station makes in
a day.

**2. One atomic mass unit.** $1.6605\times10^{-27} \text{ kg} \times (2.998\times10^8)^2 = 1.492\times10^{-10}$ J.
Dividing by $1.602\times10^{-13}$ J per MeV gives 931.5 MeV.

**3. The nuclear battery.** In a year it gives out $2{,}000 \times 3.156\times10^7 = 6.3\times10^{10}$ J. The mass lost
is $6.3\times10^{10} / (2.998\times10^8)^2 = 7\times10^{-7}$ kg: less than a milligram.

## Try it

1. Find the energy in 1 kg of mass.
2. A nuclear reaction releases 17.6 MeV. How much mass is lost, in u?
3. Why do chemical reactions not seem to change mass?
4. A reactor gives out 10 MW of heat for a year. Find the mass it loses.

??? note "Answers"
    1. $1 \times (2.998\times10^8)^2 = 9.0\times10^{16}$ J.
    2. $17.6 / 931.5 = 0.0189$ u.
    3. They release so little energy that the mass lost, $E/c^2$, is far too small to measure.
    4. $10^7 \times 3.156\times10^7 / (9.0\times10^{16}) \approx 3.5\times10^{-3}$ kg, about 3.5 g.

## Common mistake

**"Only nuclear reactions turn mass into energy."** Every energy release lowers the mass, even a burning match. It is
just that nuclear reactions release a million times more per atom, enough to measure.
