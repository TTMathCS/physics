# Absorbed dose

*Radiation and the atom · level 6, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A dose of radiation that makes a person ill puts less energy into their body than a sip of hot tea. How can so little
energy do so much harm?

## The idea

The **absorbed dose** is the radiation energy absorbed by each kilogram of material:

$$\text{absorbed dose} = \frac{\text{energy absorbed (J)}}{\text{mass (kg)}}.$$

Its unit is the **gray** (Gy): 1 Gy is 1 joule absorbed by each kilogram. Everyday doses are tiny, so they are given in
milligrays (mGy), thousandths of a gray.

A gray is very little energy as heat. The harm comes from how the energy arrives: in sharp, separate hits, each one
enough to knock electrons out of atoms and break molecules like DNA.

## Worked examples

**1. A whole body.** Suppose a person of 70 kg absorbs 1 Gy. The energy is $1 \times 70 = 70$ J.

**2. As heat.** Water needs about 4,200 J to warm 1 kg by 1 °C. The body is mostly water, so 70 J warms 70 kg by about
$70 \div (70 \times 4{,}200) \approx 0.0002$ °C. Nobody could feel that.

**3. A small dose.** A hand of 0.5 kg absorbs 0.001 J. Dose $= 0.001 \div 0.5 = 0.002$ Gy, which is 2 mGy.

## Try it

1. What does 1 gray mean?
2. A 2 kg block absorbs 0.01 J. Find the dose in mGy.
3. How much energy does a 60 kg person absorb from a dose of 3 mGy?
4. Why can a dose with so little energy be harmful?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 1 joule of radiation energy absorbed by each kilogram.
    2. $0.01 \div 2 = 0.005$ Gy, which is 5 mGy.
    3. $0.003 \times 60 = 0.18$ J.
    4. The energy comes in separate hits that each break molecules, such as DNA.

## Common mistake

**"Dose is the total energy."** Dose is energy per kilogram. A small child and an adult in the same beam get the same
dose, though the adult absorbs more energy in total.
