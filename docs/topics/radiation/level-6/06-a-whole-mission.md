# A whole mission

*Radiation and the atom · level 6, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

A dose of about 1,000 mSv can cause radiation sickness. A whole Mars mission adds up to about that much. So would the
crew come home sick?

## The idea

Add the parts. Suppose a mission takes 180 days to get there, stays 500 days, and takes 180 days to come back:

| Part of the mission | Days | Dose per day | Dose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The two trips | 360 | 1.8 mSv | 648 mSv |
| On the surface | 500 | 0.67 mSv | 335 mSv |

The total is $648 + 335 = 983$, about 980 mSv over 860 days. NASA put it the same way: on the order of **1,000 mSv** for a whole mission.

That would not make the crew sick. Radiation sickness comes from a big dose all at once, in hours or days. Spread over
more than two years, the same dose does not; the cells have time to repair. The worry is the **long-term risk**: a dose
of 1,000 mSv is linked to about a **5% higher risk of dying of cancer** later in life. In 2013, NASA's career limit for
astronauts in Earth orbit was a 3% higher risk.

| Dose over about 2.4 years | Mars mission | Earth (Canada) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural radiation | about 980 mSv | about 4 mSv |

## Worked examples

**1. At home.** 860 days at home in Canada: $860 \div 365 \times 1.8 \approx 4.2$ mSv.

**2. The risk.** 5% for each 1,000 mSv, so 980 mSv gives about $0.98 \times 5 \approx 4.9\%$ extra risk. That is
above NASA's 2013 limit of 3%.

**3. A shorter mission.** Suppose 120 days each way and 30 days on the surface:
$240 \times 1.8 + 30 \times 0.67 = 432 + 20 = 452$ mSv.

## Try it

1. Why would a 1,000 mSv mission not cause radiation sickness?
2. What is the main radiation risk of a Mars mission?
3. Find the total dose for 200 days each way and 400 days on the surface.
4. What extra cancer risk goes with a dose of 600 mSv?

??? note "Answers"
    1. It is spread over more than two years, not given all at once.
    2. A higher risk of cancer later in life.
    3. $400 \times 1.8 + 400 \times 0.67 = 720 + 268 = 988$ mSv.
    4. $0.6 \times 5 = 3\%$.

## Common mistake

**Reading the risk as a certainty.** A 5% higher risk does not mean 5% of the crew will surely get cancer from
the trip. It is an average risk over many people, and a lifetime one.
