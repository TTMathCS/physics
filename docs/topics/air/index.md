# Air and fluids

The air of Mars is about sixty times thinner than ours, and its pressure less than 1% of ours, yet it carries dust storms around the planet and let a
small helicopter fly. This topic starts with wind and bubbles and climbs to pressure, buoyancy, lift and drag, and
how to fly, land and breathe in air that thin.

8 levels. The ages are only a rough guide for parents and teachers; anyone can start at any level.

| Level | Rough age | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1: Wind and bubbles | 5-6 | Air is real stuff; feeling wind, blowing bubbles |
| Level 2: Floating and sinking | 7-8 | Boats, balloons, why some things float |
| Level 3: Air pushes | 9-10 | Air pressure, straws, suction cups, a sealed bag on a mountain |
| Level 4: Thin air | 11-12 | How thin Mars air is, why you need a pressure suit there |
| Level 5: Pressure and buoyancy | 12-13 | P = F/A, pressure with depth, Archimedes' principle |
| Level 6: Flowing fluids | 13-14 | Flow rate, Bernoulli's principle, wings and propellers |
| Level 7: Lift and drag | 15-16 | Lift and drag equations, how Ingenuity flew, dust devils |
| Level 8: Winds of a planet | 17 and beyond | Reynolds number, global winds, how dust storms grow on Mars |
