# Air has weight

*Air and fluids · level 3, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Air feels like nothing at all. But the air in your classroom weighs about as much as four grown-ups. Can that be true?

## The idea

Air is made of tiny bits, far too small to see, and each one has a little mass. Put enough of them together and the air
has real **weight**.

You can show it with a balance. Hang a blown-up balloon on each end of a stick, so the stick balances. Then pop one
balloon. The side with the full balloon drops, because the air squeezed inside it has weight.

| A room 10 m long, 8 m wide and 3 m high | Mars air | Earth air |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mass of 1 cubic metre of air | about 0.016 kg | about 1.2 kg |
| Mass of all the air in the room | about 4 kg | about 290 kg |

## Worked examples

**1. The room.** It holds $10 \times 8 \times 3 = 240$ cubic metres of air. Each cubic metre of Earth air is about
1.2 kg, so the room holds about $240 \times 1.2 = 288$ kg: about 290 kg.

**2. A litre of air.** A cubic metre holds 1,000 litres. So a litre of air is about $1.2 \div 1000$ kg, which is
1.2 g, about the mass of a paper clip.

**3. The same room full of Mars air** would hold $240 \times 0.016 = 3.84$ kg, about 4 kg. Mars air is very thin.

## Try it

1. How can you show that air has weight?
2. A cupboard holds 2 cubic metres of air. About what mass of air is that, on Earth?
3. Why does the full balloon on the balance hang lower?
4. A room holds 100 cubic metres. About what mass of Mars air would fill it?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Balance two blown-up balloons on a stick, then pop one: the full one drops.
    2. $2 \times 1.2 = 2.4$ kg.
    3. The air squeezed inside it has weight.
    4. $100 \times 0.016 = 1.6$ kg.

## Common mistake

**"Air weighs nothing."** Air is light, but not weightless. A big room's air weighs hundreds of kilograms.
