# Blowing bubbles

*Air and fluids · level 1, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

Dip a wand in soapy water and blow. Out floats a shiny, round bubble. What is inside it, and why does it pop?

## The idea

A soap bubble is a very thin skin of soapy water with **air inside**. When you blow, your breath stretches the skin
into a ball. Then the skin closes up around the air.

- Bubbles are **round**, because the stretchy skin pulls in the same on every side.
- They **drift** with the wind, because they are so light.
- They slowly **sink**, because the watery skin is a little heavier than the air.
- They **pop** when the skin gets too thin, dries out, or touches something dry.

You can also see bubbles of air in water. Blow through a straw into a glass of water: bubbles of air rush up to the top,
because air is much lighter than water.

## Worked examples

**1. A big bubble.** Blow slowly and gently, and more air goes in before the skin closes, so the bubble is bigger.

**2. A bubble that lands on a wet hand** can sit there for a while. Land it on a dry sleeve and it pops at once.

**3. Bubbles in a fish tank** come from a pump pushing air into the water. They always go up.

## Try it

1. What is inside a soap bubble?
2. Why are bubbles round?
3. Why do bubbles in water go up?
4. Give one reason a bubble pops.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Air: your breath.
    2. The stretchy skin pulls in the same on every side.
    3. Air is much lighter than water.
    4. Any one: the skin gets too thin, dries out, or touches something dry.

## Common mistake

**"Bubbles are made of soap."** The skin is soapy water, but almost all of a bubble is the air inside.
