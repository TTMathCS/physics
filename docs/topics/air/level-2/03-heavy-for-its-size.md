# Heavy for its size

*Air and fluids · level 2, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

Hold a ping-pong ball in one hand and a golf ball in the other. They are about the same size, but one feels much
heavier. Which one floats?

## The idea

What matters is not how heavy a thing is, but how **heavy it is for its size**.

Compare a thing with the **same amount of water**:

- If it is **lighter** than the same amount of water, it **floats**.
- If it is **heavier** than the same amount of water, it **sinks**.

A ping-pong ball is mostly air inside, so it is much lighter than a ball of water the same size. It floats. A golf
ball is solid, and heavier than a ball of water the same size. It sinks.

| Same size as a cup of water | Heavier or lighter than the water? | In water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a cup of cooking oil | a bit lighter | floats on top |
| a cup of wood | lighter | floats |
| a cup of ice | a bit lighter | floats |
| a cup of sand | heavier | sinks |
| a cup of metal | much heavier | sinks |

## Worked examples

**1. Ice cubes** float in a drink. Ice is a little lighter than the same amount of water, so it floats with most of it
under the surface.

**2. Oil and water.** Pour oil into water and it floats in a layer on top. It is lighter than water for its size.

**3. The log and the coin.** The log is heavy, but lighter than a log-sized lump of water, so it floats. The coin is
small, but heavier than a coin-sized drop of water, so it sinks.

## Try it

1. A ping-pong ball and a golf ball are the same size. Which floats?
2. Why does ice float?
3. You pour oil into water. Where does the oil go?
4. Is a thing that is heavy for its size likely to float or sink?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The ping-pong ball.
    2. It is a little lighter than the same amount of water.
    3. It floats on top.
    4. Sink.

## Common mistake

**"Heavy things sink."** Heavy *for their size* things sink. A big, heavy log floats because it is light for its size.
