# Level 7: Lift and drag

*Air and fluids · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

This level gives the two forces of flight their equations. You use the drag equation for cars, cyclists and falling
skydivers, and see why a parachute alone cannot land a rover on Mars. Then the lift equation shows why aircraft on Mars
must fly fast, and momentum theory gives the power Ingenuity needed to hover. Last come dust devils: spinning air with a
low-pressure core.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The drag equation](01-the-drag-equation.md) | Use $F_D = \tfrac12 \rho v^2 C_D A$, and the power needed against drag |
| [Terminal velocity](02-terminal-velocity.md) | Find the top speed of a falling object on Mars and on Earth |
| [Landing on Mars](03-landing-on-mars.md) | Explain how Perseverance shed its speed, and why it needed rockets |
| [The lift equation](04-the-lift-equation.md) | Use $L = \tfrac12 \rho v^2 C_L A$ to find the speed a wing needs |
| [How Ingenuity flew](05-how-ingenuity-flew.md) | Use momentum theory to find the power to hover |
| [Dust devils](06-dust-devils.md) | Explain how a dust devil spins up, and the pressure drop in its core |
