# Mars winds push gently

*Air and fluids · level 4, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

NASA says the strongest winds in Mars dust storms reach about 60 miles per hour, about 27 metres each second. On Earth,
that is a storm that knocks trees down. Would it knock you over on Mars?

## The idea

How hard a wind pushes depends on two things:

- the **density** of the air, and
- the **speed** of the wind, **squared**.

$$\text{push} \propto \text{density} \times \text{speed}^2.$$

"Squared" means a wind twice as fast pushes **four** times as hard, and three times as fast pushes **nine** times as
hard.

Mars air is about 75 times less dense than Earth's. So at the same speed, a Mars wind pushes about 75 times less hard.

| Same push, from | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A dust-storm wind | 27 m/s (about 97 km/h) | about 3 m/s, a light breeze |
| A usual wind | 5 m/s | about 0.6 m/s, almost still air |

## Worked examples

**1. The same speed.** A 10 m/s wind on Mars pushes about 75 times less hard than a 10 m/s wind on Earth.

**2. Finding the same push.** To make the push 75 times smaller, divide the speed by about 8.7, because
$8.7 \times 8.7 \approx 75$. So a 27 m/s Mars wind pushes like $27 \div 8.7 \approx 3$ m/s on Earth.

**3. Twice as fast.** A Mars wind that speeds up from 10 to 20 m/s pushes $2 \times 2 = 4$ times as hard.

## Try it

1. A wind triples its speed. How many times harder does it push?
2. Why does a Mars wind push less than an Earth wind at the same speed?
3. A Mars wind blows at 17 m/s. About what Earth wind would push the same?
4. Why might a light Mars wind still lift dust, even though its push is gentle?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $3 \times 3 = 9$ times.
    2. Mars air is about 75 times less dense.
    3. $17 \div 8.7 \approx 2$ m/s.
    4. Mars dust is extremely fine and light, so even a gentle push can lift it.

## Common mistake

**"Faster wind, the same push."** Doubling the speed does not double the push: it makes it four times bigger.
