# How thin is Mars air?

*Air and fluids · level 4, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

NASA describes Mars air as about 1% as dense as Earth's. What does "dense" mean, and where does that number come from?

## The idea

The **density** of a stuff is its mass in each cubic metre:

$$\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}.$$

Air at the ground on Earth has a density of about 1.2 kg/m³. Mars air at the ground is about 0.016 kg/m³. That is about
75 times less dense, or about 1.3% of Earth's. NASA often rounds it to "about 1%".

| Air at the ground | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Density | about 0.016 kg/m³ | 1.217 kg/m³ |
| Pressure | 636 Pa | 101,400 Pa |
| Air above each square metre | about 170 kg | about 10,300 kg |

Notice the density is 75 times less, but the pressure is 160 times less. They are not the same, for two reasons:
Mars air is mostly carbon dioxide, whose particles are heavier than those in Earth's air, and Mars air is colder. Both
make Mars air a bit denser than its low pressure alone would suggest.

## Worked examples

**1. How many times less dense?** $1.217 \div 0.016 \approx 76$. About 75 times.

**2. As a percentage.** $0.016 \div 1.217 \approx 0.013$, which is 1.3%.

**3. A big tent.** A Mars tent holding 50 m³ of Mars air contains $50 \times 0.016 = 0.8$ kg of it. On Earth, the same
tent holds $50 \times 1.217 \approx 61$ kg of air.

## Try it

1. What does density measure?
2. How much is 20 m³ of Earth air, in kg?
3. How much is 20 m³ of Mars air, in kg?
4. Why is Mars air's density not 160 times less than Earth's, like its pressure?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The mass in each cubic metre.
    2. $20 \times 1.217 \approx 24$ kg.
    3. $20 \times 0.016 = 0.32$ kg.
    4. Mars air is colder and made of heavier particles, so it is denser for its pressure.

## Common mistake

**"Mars air is thin because it is made of light gas."** Its particles are actually heavier than those in Earth's air.
It is thin because there is far less of it.
