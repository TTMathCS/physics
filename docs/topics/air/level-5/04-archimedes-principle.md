# Archimedes' principle

*Air and fluids · level 5, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A stone weighs 30 N in air but only 18 N when it hangs in water. Where did the other 12 N go?

## The idea

The pressure under a liquid grows with depth, so the liquid pushes harder on the **bottom** of an object than on its top.
The difference is an upward force: the **upthrust**.

**Archimedes' principle:** the upthrust on an object in a fluid equals the **weight of the fluid it pushes aside**
(displaces):

$$U = \rho_\text{fluid}\, V g,$$

where $V$ is the volume of the object under the surface.

The object's weight is $mg$. If the upthrust is less, it sinks; if the upthrust, with the object fully under, is more, it
rises and floats.

## Worked examples

**1. The stone.** It loses 12 N, so it pushes aside 12 N of water: $V = 12/(1000 \times 9.81) = 0.00122$ m³, 1.22 litres.

**2. The same stone on Mars.** Weight $30 \times 3.71/9.81 = 11.3$ N. Upthrust $1000 \times 0.00122 \times 3.71 = 4.5$
N. In water it reads $11.3 - 4.5 = 6.8$ N. Both shrink in the same proportion.

**3. In air.** The stone also displaces 1.22 litres of air: on Earth that is $1.217 \times 0.00122 \times 9.81 = 0.015$
N of upthrust. Tiny, but real.

## Try it

1. State Archimedes' principle.
2. A block of volume 0.002 m³ is fully under water on Earth. Find the upthrust.
3. A 50 N object reads 35 N when fully under water. What is its volume?
4. Find the upthrust on the block in question 2 on Mars.

??? note "Answers"
    1. The upthrust on an object in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid it displaces.
    2. $1000 \times 0.002 \times 9.81 = 19.6$ N.
    3. Upthrust 15 N, so $V = 15/(1000 \times 9.81) = 0.00153$ m³.
    4. $1000 \times 0.002 \times 3.71 = 7.4$ N.

## Common mistake

**"Upthrust depends on the object's weight."** It depends only on the volume under the surface and the fluid's density.
A lead block and a plastic block of the same size, both fully under water, feel the same upthrust.
