# Flow rate

*Air and fluids · level 6, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A Mars base needs water and air moved around it all day, through pipes and ducts. How do you work out how much flows
each second?

## The idea

The **flow rate** $Q$ is the volume passing a point each second, in m³/s. Picture fluid moving at speed $v$ through a
pipe of cross-section area $A$. In one second, a length $v$ of fluid passes, so the volume is

$$Q = A v.$$

1 m³ is 1,000 litres, so a flow rate in litres per second is 1,000 times the number in m³/s.

## Worked examples

**1. A garden hose.** Area $2.0 \times 10^{-4}$ m², water at 3 m/s: $Q = 2.0\times10^{-4} \times 3 = 6\times10^{-4}$ m³/s,
0.6 litres per second. A 10-litre bucket fills in $10/0.6 \approx 17$ s.

**2. An air duct.** A duct 0.3 m by 0.2 m carries air at 2 m/s: $Q = 0.06 \times 2 = 0.12$ m³/s.

**3. Changing the air.** A habitat room holds 60 m³. With that duct, the air is replaced every $60/0.12 = 500$ s, about
8 minutes.

## Try it

1. What does a flow rate measure?
2. Water flows at 1.5 m/s through a pipe of area $5\times10^{-4}$ m². Find $Q$ in litres per second.
3. A pipe of area $3\times10^{-4}$ m² must carry 0.9 litres per second. How fast must the water flow?
4. A round pipe is 2 cm across. Find its area, and the flow rate at 2 m/s.

??? note "Answers"
    1. The volume of fluid passing each second.
    2. $7.5\times10^{-4}$ m³/s, 0.75 litres per second.
    3. $9\times10^{-4}/3\times10^{-4} = 3$ m/s.
    4. $A = \pi \times 0.01^2 = 3.14\times10^{-4}$ m²; $Q = 6.3\times10^{-4}$ m³/s, 0.63 litres per second.

## Common mistake

**Using the diameter as the radius.** A pipe 2 cm across has a radius of 1 cm. Using 2 cm gives an area four times too
big.
