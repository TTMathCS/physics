# Narrow pipes, fast flow

*Air and fluids · level 6, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Put your thumb over the end of a garden hose and the water squirts much faster and farther. You have not turned the tap
up. Where does the extra speed come from?

## The idea

Water hardly squashes. So whatever flows into a pipe each second must flow out of it each second: the flow rate is the
same all along. This is the **continuity equation**:

$$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2.$$

Where the pipe narrows, the fluid must go **faster**. Halve the area, and the speed doubles.

The same rule works for air when its speed is well below the speed of sound, because then the air barely squashes
either.

## Worked examples

**1. The thumb.** The hose end has area $2.0\times10^{-4}$ m², with water at 3 m/s. Your thumb leaves a gap of
$0.5\times10^{-4}$ m². Speed through the gap: $3 \times 2.0/0.5 = 12$ m/s.

**2. A round pipe.** Speed goes with $1/\text{area}$, and area goes with the diameter squared. A pipe half as wide has a
quarter of the area, so the speed is four times as big.

**3. A river.** Where a river narrows between rocks, it runs fast. Where it widens into a pool, it slows down.

## Try it

1. Why must the flow rate be the same all along a pipe?
2. Water flows at 2 m/s in a pipe of area $6\times10^{-4}$ m². What is its speed where the area is $1.5\times10^{-4}$ m²?
3. A pipe narrows from 4 cm across to 1 cm across. How many times faster is the water in the narrow part?
4. Air at 2 m/s in a 0.06 m² duct passes into a 0.02 m² vent. How fast does it come out?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Water hardly squashes, so no more can go in than comes out each second.
    2. $2 \times 6/1.5 = 8$ m/s.
    3. The area is 16 times smaller, so the speed is 16 times bigger.
    4. $2 \times 0.06/0.02 = 6$ m/s.

## Common mistake

**"Half the diameter, twice the speed."** Speed depends on the *area*, which goes with the diameter squared. Half the
diameter gives four times the speed.
