# Batteries

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A torch, a toy car and a TV remote all stop working one day, until you put in new batteries. What is inside a battery?

## The idea

A battery **stores energy**, and it can push **electricity** round a loop of wire. That makes bulbs glow, motors
spin and speakers play.

- Every battery has two ends: **plus** (+) and **minus** (−). They must go in the right way round.
- Inside, there are chemicals. As the battery is used, they change, until the battery is **flat**.
- Some batteries can be charged again, like the one in a phone.

Small batteries are safe to play with. The sockets in the wall are not: never put anything into them.

## Worked examples

**1. The right way round.** In a torch, the + end of each battery points the same way as the + mark in the case.
Put one in backwards and the torch stays dark.

**2. A flat battery.** A toy car gets slower and slower, then stops. Its batteries are flat: their chemicals are used
up.

**3. Charging.** A phone plugged in at night is filling its battery with energy again.

## Try it

1. What does a battery store?
2. What are the two ends of a battery called?
3. What does it mean when a battery is flat?
4. Name something in your home with a battery you can charge again.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Energy.
    2. Plus (+) and minus (−).
    3. Its chemicals are used up, so it can no longer push electricity.
    4. Any one: a phone, a tablet, a laptop, an electric toothbrush.

## Common mistake

**"A battery is full of electricity."** It is full of chemicals that store energy. The electricity only flows when the
battery is joined into a loop.
