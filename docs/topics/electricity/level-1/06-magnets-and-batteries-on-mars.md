# Magnets and batteries on Mars

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

If you landed on Mars and got lost, could you take out a compass and find north?

## The idea

No. A compass points north on Earth because the whole Earth acts like a giant magnet. **Mars does not**, not any more.
Long ago it did, and some of its rocks are still a little magnetic, but there is no planet-wide magnet for a compass to
follow.

| Magnets | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The whole planet acts like a magnet | no, not now | yes |
| A compass points north | no | yes |

Mars has a surprise, though: its **dust sticks to magnets**. The rover Opportunity carried a small magnet, and the red
dust that landed on it gathered in a ring. Scientists found that nearly all of the dust in Mars's air is magnetic,
because it has iron in it.

And rovers run on **batteries**, just like a torch. The batteries are charged up again by sunlight on solar panels, or
by a nuclear generator that makes electricity from heat.

## Worked examples

**1. Lost on Mars.** A compass needle on Mars would not settle on north. Astronauts would find their way with maps,
landmarks and radio signals from satellites in orbit.

**2. Magnetic dust.** Hold a magnet in a pile of Mars dust and it would come out fuzzy with red grains, like a magnet in
iron filings.

**3. A rover's batteries.** Perseverance has two batteries for the busy moments of its day, charged up by its nuclear
generator.

## Try it

1. Why would a compass not point north on Mars?
2. What did Opportunity's magnet show about Mars dust?
3. What makes Mars dust stick to magnets?
4. How are a rover's batteries charged?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Mars no longer acts like a giant magnet.
    2. Nearly all of the dust in the air is magnetic.
    3. It has iron in it.
    4. By solar panels or by a nuclear generator.

## Common mistake

**"Mars has no magnets at all."** Some of its old rocks and almost all of its dust are magnetic. What Mars lacks is a
magnet the size of the whole planet.
