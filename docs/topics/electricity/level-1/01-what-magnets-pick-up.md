# What magnets pick up

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Hold a fridge magnet near a paper clip, a pencil, a spoon and a sock. Which ones jump to the magnet?

## The idea

A magnet pulls on things made of **iron** or **steel** (steel is mostly iron). It does not pull on wood, plastic,
paper, glass, cloth or rubber. It does not pull on most other metals either, like aluminium foil or copper.

- A paper clip is steel: the magnet picks it up.
- A pencil is wood: nothing happens.
- A steel spoon is pulled. A plastic spoon is not.

A magnet can even pull through paper or a thin table top. You cannot see the pull, but you can feel it.

## Worked examples

**1. A treasure hunt.** Go round the kitchen with a magnet. It sticks to the fridge door and a steel pan, but not to a
wooden spoon or a glass.

**2. Through paper.** Put a paper clip on a sheet of paper and move a magnet under the paper. The clip follows the
magnet.

**3. Not all metal.** Try a ball of aluminium foil. It is metal, but the magnet does not pull it.

## Try it

1. Will a magnet pick up a steel nail?
2. Will a magnet pick up a crayon?
3. Name two things in your home that a magnet sticks to.
4. Can a magnet pull a paper clip through a page of a book?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Yes, steel is mostly iron.
    2. No, crayons are wax.
    3. Any two: the fridge door, a steel pan, scissors, a paper clip, a steel spoon.
    4. Yes, if the magnet is strong enough and the page is thin.

## Common mistake

**"Magnets pick up all metals."** They pull iron and steel. Foil, copper wire and gold rings stay put.
