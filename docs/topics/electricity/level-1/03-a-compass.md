# A compass

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

A compass needle swings round and stops, pointing the same way every time. Turn the compass, and the needle swings back.
What is pulling it?

## The idea

A compass needle is a tiny magnet that can spin freely. It lines up with the **Earth**, because the whole Earth acts
like a giant magnet.

- The needle's north end points north.
- Once you know where north is, you can find south, east and west.

About a thousand years ago, sailors in China were already using compasses to find their way at sea, in fog and at
night. Sailors everywhere soon did the same.

Bring a magnet close to a compass and the needle turns to point at the magnet instead. So keep magnets away when you
use one.

## Worked examples

**1. Finding north.** Hold a compass flat and still. Turn your body until the needle's north end points straight ahead.
You are facing north.

**2. Behind you.** If you face north, south is behind you, east is on your right and west on your left.

**3. A fooled compass.** Move a fridge magnet round a compass. The needle follows the magnet, not the Earth.

## Try it

1. What is a compass needle?
2. Why does a compass needle point north?
3. You face north. Which way is on your left?
4. Why should you keep magnets away from a compass?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A tiny magnet that can spin.
    2. The Earth acts like a giant magnet, and the needle lines up with it.
    3. West.
    4. The needle would point at the magnet instead of north.

## Common mistake

**"A compass points to the North Pole because it is cold there."** It points along the Earth's magnetism, nothing to
do with ice or cold.
