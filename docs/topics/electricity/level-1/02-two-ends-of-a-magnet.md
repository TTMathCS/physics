# Two ends of a magnet

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Take two bar magnets. Push one end of each together, then turn one magnet round and try again. What changes?

## The idea

Every magnet has two ends, called **poles**: a **north** pole and a **south** pole. They are often painted different
colours, or marked N and S.

- A north pole and a south pole **pull together**.
- Two north poles **push apart**. So do two south poles.

The pull or push is strongest close to the poles, and it gets weaker as the magnets move apart.

## Worked examples

**1. Pull together.** Hold the north end of one magnet near the south end of another. They snap together.

**2. Push apart.** Turn one magnet round so two north ends face. You feel them push, and the loose magnet may spin
round.

**3. Floating rings.** Ring magnets on a pencil, with like poles facing, float one above the other: each pushes the
next one up.

## Try it

1. What are the two ends of a magnet called?
2. Do a north pole and a south pole pull or push?
3. Do two south poles pull or push?
4. A magnet's labels have rubbed off. How could you find its north pole using a magnet that is marked?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Poles: north and south.
    2. They pull together.
    3. They push apart.
    4. Bring the marked north pole near one end. If they push apart, that end is a north pole.

## Common mistake

**"The middle of a magnet is the strongest part."** The pull is strongest at the poles. A paper clip hardly sticks to
the middle of a bar magnet.
