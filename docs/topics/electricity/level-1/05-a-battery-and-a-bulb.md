# A battery and a bulb

*Electricity and magnetism · level 1, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

You have a battery, a small bulb and two wires. How do you join them so the bulb lights up?

## The idea

The electricity has to go **all the way round**: out of one end of the battery, along a wire, through the bulb, along
the other wire and back into the other end of the battery.

That loop is called a **circuit**. If there is a gap anywhere, the electricity stops, and the bulb goes out.

## Worked examples

**1. One wire.** With only one wire, from the battery to the bulb, nothing happens. There is no way back.

**2. A loose wire.** Lift one wire off the battery. The bulb goes out at once. Put it back and the bulb lights again.

**3. Two batteries.** Two batteries in a line, + to −, make the bulb brighter. Too many could burn it out.

## Try it

1. What do we call the loop that electricity goes round?
2. Why does one wire not light the bulb?
3. What happens if a wire comes loose?
4. A torch has a battery, a bulb and wires inside. Why does the torch need a button?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A circuit.
    2. The electricity has no way back to the battery.
    3. There is a gap, so the bulb goes out.
    4. The button closes and opens the loop, so you can turn the bulb on and off.

## Common mistake

**"The electricity gets used up in the bulb."** The electricity goes all the way round and back to the battery. What
the bulb uses is the battery's energy.
