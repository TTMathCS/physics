# Current is moving charge

*Electricity and magnetism · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A charged balloon cannot light a bulb, but a small battery can light one for hours. What is the difference?

## The idea

Static electricity is charge that has **built up and stays put**. A **current** is charge that **keeps moving**.

In a metal wire, some electrons are free to move. A battery pushes them round the circuit, all the time, so a steady
current flows. That steady flow can keep a bulb lit.

A charged balloon has a small amount of charge. If it all ran through a bulb, it would be gone in a tiny fraction of a
second, far too quick to see.

| Static electricity | Current |
| --- | --- |
| charge builds up and stays put | charge keeps flowing |
| on insulators, like a balloon | through conductors, like a wire |
| lets go in one quick spark | runs steadily, as long as the circuit is complete |

## Worked examples

**1. All at once.** When you close a switch, the bulb lights at once. The wire is already full of electrons, and they
all start moving together.

**2. A balloon on a bulb.** Touch a charged balloon to a bulb's contacts: nothing to see. Its charge is tiny next to a
battery's steady flow.

**3. Lightning.** Lightning is a huge current, but it lasts only a moment, because the cloud's charge is used up.

## Try it

1. What is the difference between static electricity and a current?
2. Why can a battery keep a bulb lit, but a balloon cannot?
3. What moves in a metal wire when a current flows?
4. Is lightning static electricity or a current?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Static is charge that stays put; a current is charge that keeps moving.
    2. A battery keeps pushing charge round; a balloon has only a little, which would be gone at once.
    3. Electrons.
    4. Both: static charge builds up in the cloud, then flows as a huge, brief current.

## Common mistake

**"Electrons rush round the circuit at the speed of light."** Each electron drifts only slowly. The bulb lights at once
because the whole wire is full of electrons that all start moving together.
