# Earth's magnetic field

*Electricity and magnetism · level 6, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A compass needle's north end points to the north. But north poles repel north poles. So what kind of pole is in the
Arctic?

## The idea

Earth's field is shaped roughly like that of a giant bar magnet inside the planet, tilted a little from the spin axis.

- A compass's north end is attracted to the pole in the Arctic. So that pole is, magnetically, a **south** pole. We
  still call it the **magnetic north pole**, because it is in the north.
- The magnetic north pole is not at the geographic North Pole, and it wanders a little each year.
- The field is not made by a solid magnet. It comes from currents in Earth's **liquid iron outer core**, kept going as
  the molten metal churns. This is called a **dynamo**.

The field reaches far out into space, where it turns aside most of the charged particles streaming from the Sun.

| Earth's field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Strength at the surface | 24 to 66 µT, weakest near the equator, strongest near the poles |
| Magnetic north pole (2020 model) | 86.5° N, 164.0° E, in the Arctic Ocean |

## Worked examples

**1. The compass.** Opposite poles attract, so the needle's north end is pulled towards the magnetic south pole that sits
in the Arctic.

**2. Strength.** 66 µT near the poles against 24 µT in the weakest places: about 2.75 times stronger.

**3. Not a solid magnet.** Iron loses its magnetism above about 770 °C, and Earth's core is far hotter. So the field
must be made by moving charge in the liquid core.

## Try it

1. What kind of magnetic pole is near the geographic North Pole?
2. What makes Earth's magnetic field?
3. Why can it not be a giant solid magnet?
4. How does Earth's field help life on the surface?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A magnetic south pole.
    2. Currents in the churning liquid iron of the outer core: a dynamo.
    3. The core is far too hot for iron to stay magnetized.
    4. It turns aside most of the charged particles streaming from the Sun.

## Common mistake

**"The magnetic and geographic north poles are the same."** The magnetic pole is hundreds of kilometres away and moves.
Navigators correct for the difference.
