# Electric fields

*Electricity and magnetism · level 6, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A charged balloon pulls on your hair from several centimetres away. How does the push or pull get across the gap?

## The idea

A charge sets up an **electric field** in the space around it. Any other charge placed in that field feels a force.
The **field strength** is the force on each coulomb:

$$E = \frac{F}{q},$$

in newtons per coulomb (N/C), which is the same as volts per metre (V/m).

**Field lines** show the field. They point the way a positive charge would be pushed: **away from +** and **towards −**.
Where the lines are closer together, the field is stronger.

Between two flat metal plates with a voltage $V$ across a gap $d$, the field is the same everywhere:

$$E = \frac{V}{d}.$$

## Worked examples

**1. The force.** A charge of $2 \times 10^{-6}$ C in a field of 5,000 N/C feels $F = qE = 0.01$ N.

**2. Two plates.** 300 V across a gap of 2 cm: $E = 300/0.02 = 15{,}000$ V/m.

**3. A spark.** Dry air near the ground on Earth breaks down at about 3,000,000 V/m. Across a 1 cm gap, that takes
$3\times10^6 \times 0.01 = 30{,}000$ V. Thin Mars air breaks down at only a few tens of thousands of V/m.

## Try it

1. A charge of $5 \times 10^{-9}$ C feels a force of $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ N. Find the field.
2. Find the field between plates 5 mm apart with 100 V across them.
3. Which way do field lines point near a negative charge?
4. Suppose Mars air breaks down at 30,000 V/m. What voltage would make a spark jump 1 cm?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $E = F/q = 30{,}000$ N/C.
    2. $100/0.005 = 20{,}000$ V/m.
    3. Inwards, towards it.
    4. $30{,}000 \times 0.01 = 300$ V.

## Common mistake

**"Field lines are paths that charges follow."** They show the direction of the **force** at each point. A moving
charge can cross field lines, the way a thrown ball does not follow the direction of gravity.
