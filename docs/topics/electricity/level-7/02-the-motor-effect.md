# The motor effect

*Electricity and magnetism · level 7, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Hang a loose wire between the poles of a strong magnet and switch on a current. The wire jumps sideways. Which way, and
how hard?

## The idea

A wire carrying a current in a magnetic field feels a force. For a straight wire of length $L$ at right angles to the
field:

$$F = BIL.$$

If the wire is at an angle $\theta$ to the field, $F = BIL\sin\theta$. Along the field, there is no force at all.

The force is at right angles to **both** the current and the field. **Fleming's left-hand rule** gives the direction:
hold your thumb, first finger and second finger at right angles.

- **F**irst finger: **F**ield (north to south).
- Se**c**ond finger: **c**urrent.
- Thu**m**b: **m**otion, the force.

## Worked examples

**1. A force.** 0.5 m of wire carries 3 A at right angles to a 0.2 T field: $F = 0.2 \times 3 \times 0.5 = 0.3$ N.

**2. At an angle.** The same wire at 30° to the field: $F = 0.3 \times \sin 30° = 0.15$ N.

**3. The direction.** In the diagram, the field points right and the current goes into the page. The left-hand rule
gives a force downwards.

## Try it

1. Find the force on 0.2 m of wire carrying 5 A at right angles to a 0.4 T field.
2. What is the force when the wire lies along the field?
3. Reverse the current in the diagram. Which way is the force?
4. A 0.1 m wire in a 0.25 T field feels 0.05 N. Find the current.

??? note "Answers"
    1. $0.4 \times 5 \times 0.2 = 0.4$ N.
    2. Zero.
    3. Upwards.
    4. $I = F/(BL) = 0.05/0.025 = 2$ A.

## Common mistake

**Using the right hand.** Fleming's rule for the motor effect uses the **left** hand. (The right hand is for
generators.)
