# Resistance

*Electricity and magnetism · level 5, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Put the same 6 V across a short thick copper wire, a bulb and a long thin pencil lead. The currents are wildly
different. What decides how much current flows?

## The idea

**Resistance** measures how hard a part makes it for current to flow through it. It is defined as

$$R = \frac{V}{I},$$

the voltage across the part divided by the current through it. Resistance is measured in **ohms** ($\Omega$): a part
has 1 $\Omega$ if 1 V drives 1 A through it.

Resistance is bigger for:

- a **longer** wire;
- a **thinner** wire;
- a material that conducts **less well** (nichrome more than copper);
- most metals, a **hotter** wire.

A **resistor** is a part made to have a set resistance, used to control the current in a circuit.

## Worked examples

**1. A resistor.** 6 V drives 0.03 A: $R = 6/0.03 = 200\ \Omega$.

**2. A bulb.** 3 V drives 0.25 A: $R = 3/0.25 = 12\ \Omega$.

**3. A thick wire.** 0.1 V drives 2 A through a short copper wire: $R = 0.1/2 = 0.05\ \Omega$, very small.

## Try it

1. 12 V drives 0.5 A through a heater element. Find its resistance.
2. Which has more resistance: a long thin wire or a short thick one, of the same metal?
3. A part has 1,000 $\Omega$. What current does 5 V drive through it?
4. What is a resistor for?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $12/0.5 = 24\ \Omega$.
    2. The long thin wire.
    3. $I = V/R = 5/1000 = 0.005$ A, 5 mA.
    4. To set or limit the current in a circuit.

## Common mistake

**"Resistance uses up current."** The current is the same going in and coming out. Resistance sets how big that current
is for a given voltage, and the part turns electrical energy into heat or light.
