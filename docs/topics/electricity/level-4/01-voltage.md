# Voltage

*Electricity and magnetism · level 4, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

An AA battery says 1.5 V. A car battery says 12 V. The socket in the wall gives 120 V. What does the V measure?

## The idea

**Voltage** measures how hard a battery or power supply pushes charge round a circuit. More exactly, it is the energy
each unit of charge carries: **1 volt (V) means 1 joule of energy for every coulomb of charge**. (A coulomb, C, is a
fixed amount of charge, the charge of about $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons.)

| Source | Voltage |
| --- | --- |
| AA or AAA cell | 1.5 V |
| Phone battery | about 3.85 V |
| Car battery | 12 V |
| Wall socket in North America | 120 V |
| Wall socket in much of the world | 230 V |

Cells joined in a line, + to −, add their voltages. Two AA cells give 3 V; four give 6 V.

## Worked examples

**1. Energy per charge.** In a 12 V circuit, each coulomb that goes round carries 12 J of energy to the parts.

**2. A torch.** Three AA cells in a line: $3 \times 1.5 = 4.5$ V.

**3. One backwards.** Four cells with one turned round: $1.5 + 1.5 + 1.5 - 1.5 = 3$ V.

## Try it

1. What does a voltage of 1 V mean?
2. How many AA cells in a line give 9 V?
3. How much energy do 5 coulombs carry from a 12 V battery?
4. Why is a wall socket dangerous when an AA cell is not?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Each coulomb of charge carries 1 joule of energy.
    2. $9/1.5 = 6$ cells.
    3. $5 \times 12 = 60$ J.
    4. It pushes about 80 times as hard (120 V against 1.5 V), enough to drive a dangerous current through a body.

## Common mistake

**"Voltage flows round the circuit."** Charge flows. The voltage is the push, or the energy each coulomb carries.
