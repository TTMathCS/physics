# Power in watts

*Electricity and magnetism · level 4, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

A kettle and an LED bulb both run from the wall socket at 120 V. One boils water in minutes, the other barely gets warm.
What is different?

## The idea

**Power** is how fast energy is delivered, in **watts** (W): 1 W is 1 joule each second.

Voltage is the energy per coulomb, and current is the coulombs per second. Multiply them and you get the joules per
second:

$$P = VI.$$

| Device | Voltage | Current | Power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kettle | 120 V | 12.5 A | 1,500 W |
| LED bulb | 120 V | 0.075 A | 9 W |
| Phone charging | 5 V | 2 A | 10 W |
| Small LED | 3 V | 0.02 A | 0.06 W |

Same voltage, very different current, very different power.

Energy is then power times time: $E = Pt$, in joules if $t$ is in seconds, or in watt-hours (Wh) if $t$ is in hours.

## Worked examples

**1. The kettle.** $P = 120 \times 12.5 = 1{,}500$ W.

**2. Finding the current.** A 60 W lamp on 120 V: $I = P/V = 60/120 = 0.5$ A.

**3. Energy.** The kettle runs for 3 minutes: $E = 1{,}500 \times 180 = 270{,}000$ J, or $1{,}500 \times 0.05 = 75$ Wh.

## Try it

1. A heater draws 10 A from 120 V. Find its power.
2. A 1,200 W hair dryer runs on 120 V. What current does it draw?
3. A phone charger gives 9 V at 2 A. What is its power?
4. A 9 W LED bulb is on for 5 hours. How many watt-hours does it use?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $120 \times 10 = 1{,}200$ W.
    2. $1{,}200/120 = 10$ A.
    3. $9 \times 2 = 18$ W.
    4. $9 \times 5 = 45$ Wh.

## Common mistake

**"Higher voltage always means more power."** Power needs both. A 120 V LED bulb uses far less power than a 12 V car
starter motor, which draws hundreds of amps.
