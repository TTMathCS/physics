# Current and amps

*Electricity and magnetism · level 4, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

A phone charger might say "2 A". A kettle draws about 12 A. What is an amp counting?

## The idea

**Current** is how much charge flows past a point each second:

$$I = \frac{Q}{t},$$

where $Q$ is the charge in coulombs and $t$ the time in seconds. Current is measured in **amps** (A):
**1 A is 1 coulomb per second**. That is about $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons every second.

| Device | Current, about |
| --- | --- |
| A small LED | 0.02 A (20 milliamps) |
| A phone charging | 2 A |
| A toaster | 7 A |
| A kettle | 12 A |

In a single loop, the current is the **same everywhere**. Charge is not used up; it goes all the way round.

## Worked examples

**1. From charge.** 30 C flow past a point in 15 s: $I = 30/15 = 2$ A.

**2. Charge from current.** A 0.5 A current runs for 2 minutes: $Q = It = 0.5 \times 120 = 60$ C.

**3. Electrons.** 1 A is $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons a second, so 2 A is $1.25 \times 10^{19}$ a second.

## Try it

1. 120 C flow in a minute. What is the current?
2. How much charge flows when 3 A runs for 10 s?
3. In a single loop, the current near the battery is 0.4 A. What is it on the far side of the bulb?
4. How long does it take 1 A to move 3,600 C?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $120/60 = 2$ A.
    2. $3 \times 10 = 30$ C.
    3. 0.4 A: the current is the same all the way round a single loop.
    4. 3,600 s, one hour.

## Common mistake

**"The bulb uses up some of the current."** The current coming out of the bulb is the same as the current going in.
The bulb takes **energy** from the charge, not the charge itself.
