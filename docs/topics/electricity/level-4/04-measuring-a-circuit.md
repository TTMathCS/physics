# Measuring a circuit

*Electricity and magnetism · level 4, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

You want to know the current through a bulb and the voltage across it. Where do you put the meters?

## The idea

- An **ammeter** measures current. It goes **in the loop** (in series), so the current flows **through** it.
- A **voltmeter** measures voltage. It goes **across** a part (in parallel), with one lead on each side, so it compares
  the two ends.

A **multimeter** can do both; you choose the setting and which sockets to use.

In a single loop, the voltages across the parts **add up** to the battery's voltage. The energy each coulomb picks up
in the battery is all handed out to the parts on its way round.

## Worked examples

**1. Two bulbs in a loop.** A 6 V battery drives two identical bulbs in one loop. A voltmeter reads 3 V across each:
$3 + 3 = 6$ V.

**2. Unequal parts.** A 9 V battery, a bulb and a motor in one loop. The bulb has 4 V across it, so the motor has
$9 - 4 = 5$ V.

**3. The ammeter.** An ammeter before the bulb and one after both read the same current, say 0.3 A.

## Try it

1. Where does an ammeter go in a circuit?
2. Where does a voltmeter go?
3. A 12 V battery runs three identical bulbs in one loop. What is the voltage across each?
4. A 4.5 V battery drives a buzzer and a bulb in one loop. The buzzer has 3 V across it. What is across the bulb?

??? note "Answers"
    1. In the loop, so the current flows through it.
    2. Across the part, one lead on each side.
    3. $12/3 = 4$ V.
    4. $4.5 - 3 = 1.5$ V.

## Common mistake

**Putting an ammeter across a part.** An ammeter lets current through very easily, so across a battery it makes a
short circuit: a huge current that can blow the meter's fuse. It must go in the loop.
