# Switches

*Electricity and magnetism · level 2, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

When you press a light switch, nothing moves that you can see, yet the light comes on. What does a switch do?

## The idea

A switch is a way to make and break a gap in a circuit.

- **Switch on (closed):** two metal pieces touch. The loop is complete and the electricity flows.
- **Switch off (open):** the metal pieces are pulled apart. There is a gap and the flow stops.

A switch can be anywhere in the loop, before or after the bulb. Either way, it opens or closes the whole loop.

## Worked examples

**1. A paper clip switch.** Push two drawing pins into card, wind the circuit wires round them, and lay a paper clip
across the pins. Lift the clip: off. Lay it down: on.

**2. A doorbell.** The button is a switch that is only closed while you press it.

**3. Where the switch goes.** Put the switch on either side of the bulb. It still turns the bulb on and off.

## Try it

1. What does a switch do to a circuit?
2. Is a switch open or closed when the light is on?
3. Does it matter which side of the bulb the switch is on?
4. Why does a doorbell stop ringing when you let go of the button?

??? note "Answers"
    1. It makes or breaks a gap in it.
    2. Closed.
    3. No, it opens or closes the whole loop either way.
    4. The button springs back, opening the gap.

## Common mistake

**"Off means the switch is closed."** On a circuit, a **closed** switch means no gap, so the bulb is **on**.
