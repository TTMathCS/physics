# Circuit symbols

*Electricity and magnetism · level 2, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

Drawing a real battery, bulb and wires takes ages. How do engineers draw a circuit quickly, so anyone in the world can
read it?

## The idea

They use **circuit symbols**: simple shapes that everyone agrees on.

- **Cell** (a single battery): a long line and a short line. The long line is the + end.
- **Battery:** two or more cells joined together.
- **Bulb:** a circle with a cross in it.
- **Switch:** a line with a gap, and a little lever that can close it.
- **Wire:** a straight line. Wires are drawn with straight edges and square corners.

## Worked examples

**1. Reading a diagram.** The diagram above shows one cell, one switch and one bulb in a single loop. With the switch
closed, the bulb lights.

**2. Drawing a torch.** Two cells, a switch and a bulb in one loop: draw two cell symbols side by side, then the switch
and the bulb.

**3. Spot the gap.** A diagram with the switch drawn open shows a circuit that is off.

## Try it

1. Which line of a cell symbol is the + end?
2. Draw the symbol for a bulb.
3. What is the difference between a cell and a battery?
4. Why do engineers use symbols instead of pictures?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The long line.
    2. A circle with a cross inside it.
    3. A cell is one; a battery is two or more cells joined together.
    4. They are quick to draw and everyone reads them the same way.

## Common mistake

**Drawing wires that do not join.** In a circuit diagram, every wire must meet the next part, with no gaps, unless you
mean an open switch.
