# Making sounds

*Waves, sound and light · level 1, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Put your fingers gently on the front of your throat and hum. What do you feel?

## The idea

You feel a buzzing. Your throat is **shaking** very fast. Every sound comes from something shaking, back and forth,
very quickly. Scientists call this shaking a **vibration**.

- Hit a drum and its skin shakes.
- Pluck a guitar string and the string shakes.
- Talk or sing, and parts of your throat shake.

When the shaking stops, the sound stops too.

## Worked examples

**1. A ruler.** Hold a ruler flat on a table with half of it sticking out over the edge. Twang the end. You can see it
shake, and you hear a sound.

**2. Stopping a sound.** Ring a bell, then hold it tightly. The bell stops shaking, and the ringing stops.

**3. A rubber band.** Stretch a rubber band over an empty box and pluck it. The band blurs as it shakes.

## Try it

1. What does every sound come from?
2. What shakes when you sing?
3. How do you stop a ringing bell?
4. Name something you can see shaking when it makes a sound.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Something shaking, or vibrating.
    2. Parts of your throat.
    3. Hold it tightly, so it stops shaking.
    4. Any one: a ruler on a table, a guitar string, a rubber band, a drum skin with rice on it.

## Common mistake

**"Sounds come from inside the drum."** The sound comes from the drum's skin shaking. Stop the skin and the sound stops.
