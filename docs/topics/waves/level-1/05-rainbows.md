# Rainbows

*Waves, sound and light · level 1, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

The Sun's light looks white. But when the Sun shines through rain, a rainbow full of colours appears. Where do the
colours come from?

## The idea

Sunlight looks white, but it is really a **mix of all the colours** together. When sunlight passes through raindrops,
the drops bend each colour by a slightly different amount, and the colours **spread out**. That is a rainbow.

The colours always come in the same order:

**red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.**

To see a rainbow, the Sun must be **behind** you and the rain **in front** of you.

## Worked examples

**1. A garden hose.** On a sunny day, stand with the Sun behind you and spray a fine mist. You can make your own
rainbow.

**2. A glass prism** spreads sunlight into the same colours, in the same order.

**3. Bubbles.** The swirly colours on a soap bubble come from sunlight too.

## Try it

1. What colour is sunlight really made of?
2. Name the colours of the rainbow in order.
3. Where must the Sun be for you to see a rainbow?
4. What colour is on the outside of a rainbow?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A mix of all the colours.
    2. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
    3. Behind you, with rain in front of you.
    4. Red.

## Common mistake

**"The raindrops are coloured."** The raindrops are clear. The colours were in the sunlight all along; the drops only
spread them out.
