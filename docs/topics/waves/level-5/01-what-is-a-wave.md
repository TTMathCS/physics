# What is a wave?

*Waves, sound and light · level 5, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A duck bobs up and down on a pond as ripples pass, but it does not move along with them. What is a wave carrying, if not
the water?

## The idea

A **wave** carries **energy** from one place to another without carrying the stuff it travels through. Each bit of water,
air or rope only moves back and forth around where it started.

There are two kinds:

- **Transverse** waves: the vibration is **at right angles** to the way the wave travels. A wave on a rope, and light.
- **Longitudinal** waves: the vibration is **along** the way the wave travels. Sound.

The parts of a wave:

- **Amplitude:** the biggest distance from the middle line. It sets how loud or bright the wave is.
- **Wavelength** $\lambda$ (lambda): the distance from one crest to the next.
- **Frequency** $f$: how many waves pass each second, in hertz.
- **Period** $T$: the time for one wave, $T = 1/f$.

## Worked examples

**1. The duck.** It moves up and down as each crest and trough passes, but stays in the same place: the energy moves on,
the water does not.

**2. A period.** A wave with $f = 50$ Hz has $T = 1/50 = 0.02$ s.

**3. Counting waves.** 12 crests pass a post in 4 seconds: $f = 12/4 = 3$ Hz.

## Try it

1. What does a wave carry?
2. Is sound a transverse or a longitudinal wave?
3. Find the period of a 200 Hz sound.
4. A wave's period is 0.5 s. What is its frequency?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Energy.
    2. Longitudinal.
    3. $1/200 = 0.005$ s.
    4. $1/0.5 = 2$ Hz.

## Common mistake

**Measuring amplitude from crest to trough.** The amplitude is from the middle line to a crest: half the crest-to-trough
height.
