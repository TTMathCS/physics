# Frequency and wavelength

*Waves, sound and light · level 5, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

An orchestra tunes to a note of 440 Hz. In Mars air, sound is slower. Would the waves of that note be longer or shorter
on Mars?

## The idea

In one second, $f$ waves pass a point, each of length $\lambda$. So the wave travels $f\lambda$ metres each second. This
is the **wave equation**:

$$v = f\lambda.$$

The frequency is set by the **source**. The speed is set by the **medium**. So when the same note moves into a slower
medium, its wavelength gets **shorter**.

| A 440 Hz note | Mars air | Earth air |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed of sound | about 240 m/s | about 340 m/s |
| Wavelength | 0.55 m | 0.77 m |

## Worked examples

**1. Earth.** $\lambda = v/f = 340/440 = 0.77$ m.

**2. Mars.** $\lambda = 240/440 = 0.55$ m.

**3. Ingenuity's hum.** At 84 Hz in Mars air: $\lambda = 240/84 = 2.9$ m.

## Try it

1. Write the wave equation and say what each letter means.
2. A wave on a rope has a frequency of 4 Hz and a wavelength of 0.5 m. Find its speed.
3. Find the wavelength of a 20 Hz sound in Earth air.
4. A sound goes from air into water, where it is about 4.4 times faster. What happens to its frequency and wavelength?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $v = f\lambda$: speed = frequency × wavelength.
    2. $4 \times 0.5 = 2$ m/s.
    3. $340/20 = 17$ m.
    4. The frequency stays the same; the wavelength becomes about 4.4 times longer.

## Common mistake

**"A higher frequency makes the wave go faster."** In one medium, all frequencies travel at about the same speed. A
higher frequency gives a shorter wavelength, not a faster wave.
