# How we see

*Waves, sound and light · level 2, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

You are reading a book by a lamp. The book does not shine. So how does the picture of the page get into your eyes?

## The idea

You see something when **light from it enters your eyes**.

- For a light source, like a lamp, the light goes straight from the lamp into your eyes.
- For everything else, light from a source falls on the thing, **bounces off** it, and some of that bounced light
  enters your eyes.

So the path is: **lamp → book → eye**. Your eyes do not send anything out. They catch light.

## Worked examples

**1. In the dark.** Switch off the lamp and you cannot see the book, because no light is bouncing off it.

**2. The Moon.** Light goes Sun → Moon → your eyes.

**3. A rover's camera** works like an eye: it catches light that has bounced off Mars rocks.

## Try it

1. What must happen for you to see something?
2. Draw arrows to show how you see a cat under a street light.
3. Why can't you see in a completely dark room?
4. Does light go out of your eyes when you look at something?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Light from it must enter your eyes.
    2. Street light → cat → your eyes.
    3. There is no light to bounce off things into your eyes.
    4. No. Light comes into your eyes.

## Common mistake

**"Eyes send out beams to see."** Eyes only catch light. The arrows always point **into** the eye.
