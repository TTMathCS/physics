# Shadows

*Waves, sound and light · level 2, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

In the middle of a sunny day your shadow is short. In the evening it stretches out long across the ground. Why does a
shadow change?

## The idea

A **shadow** forms when something **blocks** light. Light travels in straight lines, so behind the object there is a
patch the light cannot reach. That dark patch is the shadow, and it has the same outline as the object.

- **Opaque** things, like wood or your hand, block light and make dark shadows.
- **Translucent** things, like tissue paper, let some light through and make pale shadows.
- **Transparent** things, like clear glass, let almost all the light through and make hardly any shadow.

The **closer** the object is to the lamp, the **bigger** its shadow. Move it towards the screen and the shadow shrinks.

## Worked examples

**1. Shadow puppets.** Hold your hand close to a torch and its shadow fills the wall. Move it near the wall and the
shadow is small and sharp.

**2. The Sun's height.** When the Sun is high, at midday, shadows are short. When it is low, in the morning or evening,
shadows are long.

**3. A sundial** uses the shadow of a stick to show the time as the Sun moves across the sky.

3. How can you make a shadow puppet bigger?
4. When are shadows longest?

    1. Something blocking light.
    2. No. It lets almost all the light through.
    3. Move it closer to the lamp.
    4. In the morning and evening, when the Sun is low.

## Common mistake

**"A shadow is a reflection."** A shadow is a patch where light is missing, because it was blocked. A reflection is
light bouncing back.
