# Sound is a vibration

*Waves, sound and light · level 3, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

Sprinkle some rice on a drum and tap it. The rice jumps. Now hold a speaker playing music close to a candle flame, and
the flame flickers. What is the sound doing to the air?

## The idea

Air is made of tiny particles. When a drum skin moves out, it **squashes** the air particles in front of it together.
When it moves back, it leaves a **stretched-out** patch where they are spread apart.

Each squashed patch pushes on the next air along, and that pushes on the next. So a pattern of **squashes and
stretches** travels out from the drum. This travelling pattern is a **sound wave**.

The air particles themselves only jiggle back and forth a tiny bit. It is the pattern, and the energy, that travels.

## Worked examples

**1. The rice.** The drum skin shakes up and down, and the rice is thrown into the air.

**2. The candle.** The squashes and stretches from the speaker push the air near the flame back and forth, and it
flickers.

**3. A spring.** Push and pull one end of a long, loose spring. You can see squashed bunches travelling along it, just
like a sound wave.

## Try it

1. What does a vibrating drum do to the air next to it?
2. What travels along in a sound wave?
3. Do the air particles travel all the way from the drum to your ear?
4. How can you show sound waves with a long spring?

??? note "Answers"
    1. It squashes and stretches it, over and over.
    2. A pattern of squashes and stretches, carrying energy.
    3. No. They only jiggle back and forth; the pattern travels.
    4. Push and pull one end, and squashed bunches travel along the spring.

## Common mistake

**"Sound is air blowing from the drum to you."** No air rushes across the room. Each bit of air only jiggles a tiny
amount and passes the push on.
