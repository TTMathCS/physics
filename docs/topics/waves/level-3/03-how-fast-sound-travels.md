# How fast sound travels

*Waves, sound and light · level 3, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

In a thunderstorm you see the lightning flash, then hear the thunder a few seconds later. They happen at the same
moment. Why does the thunder arrive late?

## The idea

Sound is fast, but **light is far faster**. The flash reaches you almost at once. The sound takes a few seconds.

In air at about 15 °C, sound travels about **340 metres every second**. That is about **1 kilometre in 3 seconds**.

| Speed of sound | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In the air at the ground | about 240 m/s | about 340 m/s |
| Time to travel 1 km | about 4.2 s | about 2.9 s |

Sound goes faster through liquids and solids: about 1,500 m/s in water, and about 5,000 m/s in steel.

## Worked examples

**1. How far is the storm?** You count 6 seconds between the flash and the thunder. Each 3 seconds is about 1 km, so the
storm is about 2 km away.

**2. An echo.** You shout at a cliff 170 m away. The sound goes there and back, 340 m, so the echo comes after about
1 second.

**3. On Mars.** Sound is slower, about 240 m/s. A sound from 1 km away takes about $1000 \div 240 \approx 4.2$ seconds.

## Try it

1. Why do you see lightning before you hear thunder?
2. You count 9 seconds between the flash and the thunder. About how far away is the storm?
3. An echo comes back after 2 seconds. How far away is the cliff?
4. Does sound travel faster in air or in water?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Light travels much faster than sound.
    2. About 3 km.
    3. The sound travels 680 m there and back, so the cliff is 340 m away.
    4. In water.

## Common mistake

**Forgetting that an echo goes there and back.** The echo's time is for the trip to the cliff **and** back, so the
cliff is only half the distance the sound travelled.
