# Pitch

*Waves, sound and light · level 3, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

A dog whistle makes a sound that dogs can hear but people cannot. How can a sound be too high to hear?

## The idea

The number of vibrations each second is the **frequency**. It is measured in **hertz** (Hz): 1 Hz is one vibration
each second.

- High frequency → **high** pitch.
- Low frequency → **low** pitch.

Young people can usually hear from about **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz**. Above that is **ultrasound**, too high for us but not
for dogs and bats. Below it is **infrasound**, too low for us, but used by elephants.

| Sound | Frequency, about |
| --- | --- |
| Lowest note we can hear | 20 Hz |
| The A that orchestras tune to | 440 Hz |
| Highest note we can hear | 20,000 Hz |

On Mars, Perseverance's microphone heard the Ingenuity helicopter hum at about 84 Hz.

## Worked examples

**1. A guitar string** vibrating 110 times a second makes a 110 Hz note, the low A on a guitar.

**2. Tightening a string** makes it vibrate faster, so its pitch goes up.

**3. Ingenuity's hum.** Each blade passing overhead gives a little push to the air. On Mars, Perseverance's microphone
recorded the helicopter's hum at about 84 Hz, a low note.

## Try it

1. What is frequency?
2. What unit is frequency measured in?
3. Can people hear a sound at 30,000 Hz?
4. A string vibrates 220 times a second. What is its frequency?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The number of vibrations each second.
    2. Hertz (Hz).
    3. No. It is ultrasound, above the top of our hearing.
    4. 220 Hz.

## Common mistake

**"A higher frequency means a faster sound."** All sounds in the same air travel at the same speed. A higher frequency
means more vibrations each second, not a faster wave.
