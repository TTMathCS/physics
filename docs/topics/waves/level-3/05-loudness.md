# Loudness

*Waves, sound and light · level 3, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

A whisper and a jet engine are both sounds. A jet engine can damage your hearing. How do we measure how loud a sound is?

## The idea

A louder sound comes from a **bigger** vibration. The air is squashed and stretched more, and the wave carries more
energy. The size of the vibration is the **amplitude**.

We measure loudness in **decibels** (dB). The scale is unusual: every **10 dB** more means the sound carries **10 times**
as much energy.

| Sound | Loudness, about |
| --- | --- |
| Rustling leaves | 20 dB |
| A whisper | 30 dB |
| Talking | 60 dB |
| A busy road | 80 dB |
| A rock concert | 110 dB |
| A jet engine close by | 140 dB: painful |

Long times above about 85 dB can damage your hearing.

## Worked examples

**1. 10 dB more.** A sound of 70 dB carries 10 times the energy of a sound of 60 dB.

**2. 20 dB more.** Two steps of 10 dB: $10 \times 10 = 100$ times the energy.

**3. On Mars.** Scientists found that the same source makes sounds about 20 dB weaker on Mars than on Earth: about a
hundredth of the energy reaching your ear.

## Try it

1. What is the amplitude of a sound?
2. What unit is loudness measured in?
3. How many times more energy does a 90 dB sound carry than a 60 dB sound?
4. Why should you be careful with loud music in headphones?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The size of its vibration.
    2. Decibels (dB).
    3. Three steps of 10 dB: $10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1{,}000$ times.
    4. Long times above about 85 dB can damage your hearing.

## Common mistake

**"Twice the decibels, twice as loud."** Decibels go up by 10 for every 10 times the energy. 120 dB is not twice 60 dB:
it carries a million times the energy.
