# Colour cameras on Mars

*Waves, sound and light · level 6, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

NASA's notes on Curiosity's sunset photo say the colours were "calibrated and white-balanced". If a camera just records
light, why do the colours need adjusting?

## The idea

A digital camera has millions of tiny light sensors, **pixels**. On their own, they only measure brightness. To record
colour, each pixel sits under a tiny **filter**: red, green or blue. The camera combines the three to make each colour.

But the camera's filters do not exactly match human eyes. NASA notes that Curiosity's Mastcam sees colour very like
people do, but is a little **less sensitive to blue**. And the light on Mars is itself different: dimmer, and tinted by
the dust.

So scientists **calibrate** images. Perseverance carries a calibration target with colour and grey swatches; the
cameras photograph it, and the team uses the known colours to fine-tune the settings. **White balance** is one adjustment: it shifts the colours so that a white object looks white, as it would
under sunlight on Earth. This helps geologists compare Mars rocks with rocks on Earth.

## Worked examples

**1. Mixing.** A pixel area that reads strong red, medium green and weak blue makes an orange colour.

**2. White balance.** If a white target looks slightly yellow in a Mars image, the software turns down red and green a
little, or turns up blue, until the target looks white.

**3. Two versions.** Scientists may make a "natural" image, close to what a person on Mars would see, and a
white-balanced one, showing the rocks as they would look on Earth.

## Try it

1. Why does each pixel need a colour filter?
2. What does white balance do?
3. Why might a camera image of Mars look slightly different from what a person would see?
4. Why do geologists like white-balanced images?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A pixel on its own measures only brightness, not colour.
    2. It adjusts the colours so that white objects look white.
    3. The camera's filters do not exactly match human eyes; Mastcam, for example, is a little less sensitive to blue.
    4. They can compare the colours of Mars rocks with rocks on Earth.

## Common mistake

**"Adjusted colours are fake."** Calibration corrects for the camera and the lighting. It is done carefully, with
targets of known colour, to show the true colours of the rocks.
