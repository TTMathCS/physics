# Converging lenses

*Waves, sound and light · level 7, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

A magnifying glass can focus sunlight to a tiny bright spot. How does a curved piece of glass bring light together?

## The idea

A **converging** (convex) lens is thicker in the middle. Rays parallel to its axis bend towards the axis and meet at
the **principal focus** $F$. The distance from the lens to $F$ is the **focal length** $f$. A fatter, more curved lens
bends more and has a shorter focal length.

The **power** of a lens, in **dioptres** (D), is

$$P = \frac{1}{f}, \quad f \text{ in metres}.$$

To find an image, draw two rays from the top of the object:

1. A ray **parallel** to the axis bends through $F$ on the far side.
2. A ray through the **centre** of the lens goes straight on.

Where they cross is the top of the image.

When the object is further than $2f$ away, as here, the image is **real** (light really meets there), **upside down**
and **smaller**. That is how a camera or an eye works. If the object is closer than $f$, the rays never meet on the far
side, and you see a **virtual**, upright, magnified image: a magnifying glass.

## Worked examples

**1. Power.** A lens with $f = 0.25$ m has $P = 1/0.25 = 4$ D.

**2. Focal length.** A lens of power 10 D has $f = 1/10 = 0.1$ m.

**3. Burning spot.** Sunlight arrives as parallel rays, so a magnifying glass focuses it at its focal point.

## Try it

1. What is the focal length of a lens?
2. Find the power of a lens with a focal length of 50 cm.
3. Which ray is easiest to draw through the centre of a lens, and why?
4. Where must an object be for a converging lens to act as a magnifying glass?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The distance from the lens to its principal focus.
    2. $1/0.5 = 2$ D.
    3. The ray through the centre: it goes straight on, because the two faces there are almost parallel.
    4. Closer to the lens than its focal length.

## Common mistake

**"A real image is one you can see."** A real image is one where light actually meets, so it can be caught on a screen.
You can see a virtual image too, through the lens, but it cannot be caught on a screen.
