# The lens equation

*Waves, sound and light · level 7, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

A camera lens has a focal length of 50 mm. To photograph a rock 2 m away, how far behind the lens must the sensor be?

## The idea

For a thin lens of focal length $f$, an object at distance $u$ forms an image at distance $v$, where

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}.$$

(Here real distances are positive. A negative $v$ means a virtual image, on the same side as the object.)

The **magnification** is the image height over the object height:

$$m = \frac{v}{u}.$$

| Object distance $u$ | Image |
| --- | --- |
| Very far away | at $f$, tiny, upside down |
| Beyond $2f$ | between $f$ and $2f$, smaller, upside down |
| At $2f$ | at $2f$, same size, upside down |
| Between $f$ and $2f$ | beyond $2f$, bigger, upside down |
| Closer than $f$ | virtual, upright, bigger |

## Worked examples

**1. The camera.** $1/v = 1/0.050 - 1/2 = 20 - 0.5 = 19.5$, so $v = 0.0513$ m, 51.3 mm. Magnification
$0.0513/2 = 0.026$: a 1 m rock makes a 26 mm image.

**2. A projector.** A slide 0.11 m from a lens of $f = 0.10$ m: $1/v = 10 - 9.09 = 0.91$, so $v = 1.1$ m. Magnification
$1.1/0.11 = 10$.

**3. A magnifier.** An object 0.04 m from a lens of $f = 0.05$ m: $1/v = 20 - 25 = -5$, so $v = -0.2$ m, virtual.
Magnification $0.2/0.04 = 5$.

## Try it

1. A lens has $f = 0.20$ m. An object is 0.60 m away. Find $v$ and the magnification.
2. Where must an object be placed to give a same-size image with a lens of $f = 0.15$ m?
3. A camera with $f = 0.10$ m photographs a distant mountain. Where is the image?
4. A lens of $f = 0.10$ m makes a virtual image 0.30 m from the lens. How far away is the object?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $1/v = 5 - 1.667 = 3.333$: $v = 0.30$ m; $m = 0.30/0.60 = 0.5$.
    2. At $2f = 0.30$ m.
    3. At the focal point, 0.10 m behind the lens.
    4. $1/u = 1/0.10 - 1/(-0.30) = 10 + 3.33 = 13.33$: $u = 0.075$ m.

## Common mistake

**Adding the distances instead of their reciprocals.** The equation adds $1/u$ and $1/v$. Work out $1/v$ first, then
turn it upside down.
