# Talking on Mars

*Waves, sound and light · level 4, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

Imagine two astronauts on Mars, each with a speaker on the outside of their helmet. One plays a recording of their
voice. What would the other hear?

## The idea

Put together what you have learned:

1. **Quieter.** The speaker sends about 20 dB less sound energy into Mars air: a hundredth as much.
2. **Muffled.** The high parts of speech, which make sounds like "s", "t" and "k" clear, fade quickly in Mars air.
3. **Slower.** The sound takes longer to arrive: at 240 m/s instead of 340 m/s.
4. **The same pitch.** The frequency is set by the speaker, so the voice would not sound higher or lower.

So a voice would sound faint and muffled, and hard to understand beyond a short distance. Astronauts would talk by
radio instead.

| A voice played from a speaker, 5 m away | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How loud | about 20 dB quieter | normal |
| High parts like "s" and "t" | fade fast | clear |
| Time to arrive | 0.021 s | 0.015 s |
| Pitch | the same | the same |

Inside a habitat, with air at a pressure like Earth's, voices would sound normal again.

## Worked examples

**1. Arrival time on Mars.** $5/240 = 0.021$ s.

**2. On Earth.** $5/340 = 0.015$ s.

**3. Pitch.** A voice at 200 Hz stays at 200 Hz; the air does not change the frequency of a wave passing through it.

## Try it

1. Give three ways a voice would sound different in Mars air.
2. Why would the pitch of the voice not change?
3. Why would words be hard to understand at a distance?
4. How would astronauts on Mars really talk to each other outside?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Any three: quieter, muffled, slower to arrive.
    2. The frequency is set by the vibrating source, not the air.
    3. The high parts of speech, which make words clear, fade quickly.
    4. By radio.

## Common mistake

**"Voices on Mars would sound squeaky or deep."** Pitch depends on the frequency of the source, which the air does not
change. Voices would be quieter and muffled, not higher or lower.
