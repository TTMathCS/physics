# Emission lines

*Waves, sound and light · level 8, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Look at a glowing tube of hydrogen through a spectroscope and you do not see a rainbow. You see four thin, bright lines:
red, blue-green, blue and violet. Where exactly are they?

## The idea

A hot, thin gas gives out light only at the wavelengths of its electron jumps: an **emission spectrum** of bright lines.

For hydrogen, every jump down to $n = 2$ gives a visible or near-ultraviolet line. These are the **Balmer lines**, and
their wavelengths follow the **Rydberg formula**:

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right), \qquad R = 1.097\times10^7 \text{ m}^{-1}.$$

| Jump | Wavelength | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 → 2 | 656.3 nm | red |
| 4 → 2 | 486.1 nm | blue-green |
| 5 → 2 | 434.0 nm | blue |
| 6 → 2 | 410.2 nm | violet |

The pattern is a fingerprint. Find these four lines in the light from a star, a nebula or a hot spark, and you know
hydrogen is there.

## Worked examples

**1. The red line.** $1/\lambda = 1.097\times10^7 \times (0.25 - 0.1111) = 1.524\times10^6$ m⁻¹, so $\lambda = 656$ nm.

**2. The series limit.** As $n \to \infty$, $1/\lambda \to R/4$, so $\lambda \to 4/R = 365$ nm: the lines crowd together
towards 365 nm.

**3. Sodium** has a pair of strong yellow lines at 589.0 and 589.6 nm, close together.

## Try it

1. What does an emission spectrum look like?
2. Use the Rydberg formula to find the wavelength of the $7 \to 2$ line.
3. Why do hydrogen's Balmer lines get closer together at shorter wavelengths?
4. A spark from a rock shows lines at 656.3 nm and 486.1 nm. What element do they show?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Bright lines at particular wavelengths, on a dark background.
    2. $1/\lambda = 1.097\times10^7 \times (0.25 - 0.0204) = 2.519\times10^6$ m⁻¹: $\lambda = 397$ nm.
    3. The higher energy levels get closer together, so the jumps from them to $n = 2$ differ less.
    4. Hydrogen.

## Common mistake

**"Each element gives one colour."** Each element gives a set of lines. Sodium looks yellow because its two strongest
lines are yellow, but it has many others.
